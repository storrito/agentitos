# Hooks, endings, on-screen text

From an audit of the pilot's six strongest against its six weakest posts (same lane mix), plus platform research.
The pilot now runs these rules as one package; until its before/after verdict the audit findings count as
*observed*, not *measured*.

## The first second

- **The first frame proves the first sentence.** In the tops the claim was visible in frame 0–1: the thing the
  sentence names fills the frame, doing what the sentence says. In the flops the sentence claimed what nobody
  could see yet: a size the frame cut off, a subject that entered only after a second and a half, a black fade-in.
- Choose the hook shot first, then write the sentence for it.
- **A contradiction, a secret, something impossible**, not a spec, a price or a superlative.
- **Never "Remember the …?"** (pilot, measured: 0 breakouts in 7).
- Never start on the source's fade-in or transition: check frame 0 for darkness.
- **A text hook from frame 0:** 2–5 words, upper third, ~2.5 s, the same words as the cover. It works on mute;
  word-by-word captions only show fragments.
- **Make the subject big.** Landscape footage on a vertical canvas often leaves the subject at 10–30 % of the
  frame; punch in on the hook shot (pilot: 1.3× inside a blurred-fill frame).

## The end

- **No end card.** The pilot's 4.3 s card was 9–10 % of a 45 s video and 16–20 % of a 25 s one, exactly where
  platforms measure watching to the end, and the closing question already ran over it. End on action ~0.7 s after
  the last word; the handle can sit on screen during the last seconds.
- **Loop:** the second-to-last sentence leads back to the image of the first one, so the replay feels seamless.
  Then the ending of the running test (`followers.md`).

## Own graphics

- An own graphic layer makes a faceless account recognisable *and* original. Example: a **SIZE CHECK** card, bars
  comparing the subject with things everyone knows (a Boeing 747, a blue whale, a football pitch), whenever size,
  mass, speed or power is the aha. Every reference value is sourced like any fact.
- Short chips (≲ 25 characters), and all text inside the area no app covers: x 120–960, from y 840 x 120–888,
  y 288–1248 (`safe-zones.md`). TikTok covers far more than Instagram.

## Not the lever (pilot, measured)

- Faster cutting: posts with ≥ 3 clips per 10 s had a *lower* median, by about 30 %.
- Hook type labels alone (number, superlative, "This is …"): no difference.

## Length (hypothesis)

Large studies put 30–60 s in the weakest band (Socialinsider 2026: 4.2 % engagement vs. 6.0 % at 15–30 s and
4.8 % at 60–90 s; Buffer: videos over 60 s +43 % reach). That is correlation, so the pilot tests it: a 20–28 s cut
against a 45–50 s cut on TikTok, alternating per post. Treat no length as proven before its verdict.
