# captions

Word-timed captions for videos: each word appears as it is said, in one of several **styles**, the key words in a colour of their own, placed clear of what TikTok, Reels, Shorts and the other apps lay over the video (agentitos' platform-zones), in a brand's colour and font when there is a brand kit. The captions are a [HyperFrames](https://hyperframes.heygen.com) sub-composition that any host composition mounts as one clip, so the person sees them in the player before anything is rendered; an `.srt` for the platforms' own captions comes beside the video.

It is an agent of its own (upload a video, get it back with captions, see [SKILL.md](SKILL.md)) and the captions step of the other agents of this collection: a skill with its own timed words (best-takes: the cut's words, timed in the cut) calls `build` and mounts the result.

```
node tools/captions.mjs styles
node tools/captions.mjs styles --json                                     # the same for a skill that sets captions with this one: each style's words and lines a group
node tools/captions.mjs transcribe <video> <words.json>
node tools/captions.mjs mark <words.json> [--product "Storrito"]
node tools/captions.mjs box <words.json> "📏 32 centimetres wide"     # only when cards are asked for: a phrase as a box of its own
node tools/captions.mjs build <words.json> <composition>/captions --style hormozi [--size 1080x1920] [--platform reels,tiktok] [--brand brand/DESIGN.md]
node tools/captions.mjs build <words.json> <composition>/captions --style clean --size 1920x1080 --x 704 --y 930 --max-width 1120 --align left   # another place than the safe box's bottom
node tools/captions.mjs project <video> <words.json> <composition> --style karaoke
node tools/captions.mjs srt <words.json> <file.srt>
node --test tools/test.mjs                                           # where the captions go, the styles, and that nothing changed for captions nobody placed; no request to a model (its last two tests want a Chrome and Google's fonts, else they are skipped)
```

Needs Node 20+, agentitos' [platform-zones](../platform-zones/) beside it (where the captions go) and, for `transcribe`, agentitos' [transcribe](../transcribe/) (or `TRANSCRIBE` naming its `tools/transcribe.mjs`), ffmpeg (for `transcribe`, `project`) and OpenRouter (for `transcribe`, `mark`): `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, or on a Sprite the gateway's `openrouter` connection (`tools/lib/openrouter.mjs` asks `http://sprite.internal/v1/gateway/list`); no npm packages. The models are OpenRouter's only.

## Words

`words.json` is `{"width": 1080, "height": 1920, "duration": 61.2, "words": [{"text": "Hello", "start": 0.12, "end": 0.48, "highlight": true}, …]}`, or the array of words alone; times in seconds, in the video the captions go on (after every cut: a skill that cuts maps its words onto the cut first).

- **`transcribe`** takes the words from **ElevenLabs Scribe v2** through OpenRouter, run by agentitos' [transcribe](../transcribe/) skill (`tools/lib/transcribe.mjs` finds it; the same words as best-takes': 10-minute pieces with an overlap, 20 ms timings, about half a cent a minute). Fillers ("äh", "um") and noises stay out of the captions. Three or more words in a row squeezed into under 60 ms each are left out too: Scribe now and then writes a sentence twice and the copy lands in such a run, which would show the sentence twice.
- A token without a letter or a digit ("…", "–", "&", a quotation mark the recognizer set apart) is no caption of its own: it joins a word said within 0.6 s of it, and a mark with no word near it, such as a "…" for a pause, is left out, in the picture and in the subtitle file (`fold` in `tools/lib/group.mjs`). A mark that opens or stands before its word goes with the word after it (an opening bracket, "§ 202a", "#1", "@name", a currency sign before a number, and the first of every pair of double quotation marks, whatever their shape); any other goes with the word before it. A mark never crosses the edge of a card, and a `highlight` or `icon` it carried goes to the word it joined. best-takes and screencast-cut write their own subtitle files: they leave a soft hyphen out too, but keep a lone mark as the recognizer gave it.
- **`mark`** sends the numbered words to **Claude Haiku 5.5** through OpenRouter (`--model` for another) and sets `"highlight": true` on the few that should stand out: numbers with their units, product and brand names (`--product` names the one that matters), the key word of a claim or a punchline, a call to action; about one word in eight, never two neighbours. This is what Storrito's [content workflow](https://github.com/storrito/tool-content-workflow) does with its transcript (`:highlight? true`), here with an OpenRouter model.
- **`box`** makes spoken phrases cards of their own, for whoever asks for them: `box <words.json> "📏 32 centimetres wide" "🛰️ Space Station"`. A phrase is words as they are said (the first place it is said and is not yet in a box); a first token without a letter or a digit is its emoji. The words get the same `"box"`, the first the `"icon"`. In the captions such a run is one group whatever the style's size, on a dark card with a border in the style's key colour, the emoji in front, its numbers in the key colour, the word being said marked as the style does it. A phrase is one card: said again with `box`, its card stays and only its emoji changes. `--drop "<phrase>"` takes one card away, `--clear` all; without phrases the boxes there are listed; a phrase that could not be boxed is named and the tool ends with 1. **Nothing is boxed unless it is asked for:** words without `box` are grouped exactly as before (checked against the grouping of the version before, 1,600 random word lists over the four styles), so captions built by other skills do not change. The emoji needs a colour-emoji font on the machine that renders (the Sprites have Noto Color Emoji, seen in a preview on 2026-11-12); a card without an emoji needs none.

## Styles

Styles are data (`tools/lib/styles.mjs`): how words group, how they look, how they move. A new look is a new entry. `styles` lists them; [references/styles.jpg](references/styles.jpg) shows each on the same frame, and [references/styles.md](references/styles.md) says which fits which kind of video.

| style | look | group | motion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hormozi` | Montserrat 900, capitals, white with a black stroke; the word being said yellow and a little bigger, key words green | 1–3 words | the group punches in (there at once, a little smaller, snapping to its size), the active word lights up, the group cuts out |
| `karaoke` | Poppins 800, sentence case; the word being said on a violet pill, key words yellow | 3–4 words | the group rises in, the pill moves from word to word, the group fades |
| `wipe` | Poppins 800, capitals; each word wiped in as it is said, said words dim, key words gold | up to 4 words | after Storrito's `caption-clip-wipe` ([tool-shortform-subtitles](https://github.com/storrito/tool-shortform-subtitles)) |
| `clean` | Inter 600, sentence case, on a soft dark box | whole phrases, up to 2 lines | fade in and out |
| `bold` | Montserrat 700, sentence case, white with a soft shadow, nothing behind; key words yellow | 2–3 words | cut in, cut out, nothing moves |
| `outline` | Montserrat 900, sentence case, white with a thick black outline and a drop shadow; key words yellow | 1–3 words | the group punches in (there at once, a little smaller, snapping to its size) and cuts out |
| `impact` | League Gothic, capitals with a dark edge; a caption that holds a key word stands whole in lime | 2–3 words | cut in, cut out |
| `single` | Montserrat 900, sentence case, big, a heavy shadow; key words yellow | one word; a word that would be on screen for under 0.15 s shares its caption with the next in its phrase, else with the one before ("für die", "ist dann"): three words at most, and only where they set in the line at 85 % of the size or more ("die" stays alone before "Digitalisierung") | each caption punches in and stays until the next is said |
| `fill` | Poppins 800, sentence case; the words wait dim, the one being said lights up yellow and stays white | 4–5 words, 2 lines | the group rises in, fills word by word, fades |
| `label` | Poppins 600, sentence case, each line on a white box of its own; the words wait grey and turn black, key words blue | 4–5 words, 2 lines | cut in, fills word by word, cut out |
| `editorial` | Inter 500, sentence case, soft shadow; key words in a larger Playfair Display italic, cream | 3–5 words, 2 lines | fade in and out |
| `subtitle` | Inter 600, sentence case, white with a thin dark edge, no box; key words yellow | whole phrases, up to 2 lines | cut in, cut out |

**Where the eight after `clean` come from** (2026-11-17): 198 published Shorts with captions (talking heads, podcast clips, screens with a face, narrated videos), each one's captions classed by look from strips of its frames. 147 carried a look the four first styles could not make by changing colours or a font: bold words with nothing behind them (49, the commonest look of all), phrases without a box (28), tall narrow capitals whose captions change colour (19), a box for every line (12), heavy outlined sentence case (11), a serif or a second typeface for the key words (10), words that wait dim and fill as they are said (6); 17 showed one word at a time. Looks the caption tools advertise but the Shorts did not carry (neon glow: 2, typewriter: 0, emoji: 0) are not built. What a style needs beyond colours, a font and sizes is a key in its entry and a few lines in `tools/lib/render.mjs` (the script is the same in every built file and asks the look; only the style rules differ): `lineBox` (once a group is fitted, a box is laid behind every line of its text, a word that breaks inside itself having two: as high as the line and as wide as its text, sized by the line and not by the font, so a brand's font sits in it as the style's does), `keyFont` (key words in another typeface, larger; their lines keep the pitch of the others), `groupKey` (a caption that holds a key word stands whole in the key colour), `active: "fill"` (the words wait in `wait`, take the accent while they are said and stay in `color`), `in: "punch"` (a pop without the frame in which the group is half transparent: a word of a tenth of a second has three frames at 30 fps), `gap: 0` in the group (a caption that is cut in and cut out ends in the moment the next one starts; with the usual 20 ms between them, three in five changes show a frame without any caption at 30 fps). Three of the four first styles took these over afterwards ("The first four, looked at again" below).

**The first four, looked at again** (2026-11-18). No two of the twelve are one look: by seven traits (words at once, case, typeface, edge or box, what the word being said does, how key words stand out, how a caption comes) the nearest pairs differ in two, `karaoke` and `fill` (a pill, or dim to bright), `clean` and `subtitle` (a box, or a thin edge), `outline` and `single` (two or three words, or one); `hormozi` and `outline` share typeface and outline and differ in three (capitals and a lit word, or sentence case and calm). All four stay: `clean` is the second most common look of the 198 Shorts (44), `hormozi` the look people ask for by name and the default of the skills that call this one, `karaoke` the only style with a box that moves from word to word (8), `wipe` the only one whose words appear as they are said (1 of the 198 wiped, 7 more built their line word by word). What the new styles had that three of them lacked, they got:

- `hormozi`: its captions popped in from transparent and ended 20 ms before the next, so at most changes one frame showed no caption at all and the next a half-transparent one. They now end as the next begins and punch in opaque (`gap: 0`, `in: "punch"`), and phrases are cut by length. The word being said grows by 8 %: in a full line it reached 22 px past the area on each side, so a line now keeps half the growth as room at its sides.
- `karaoke`: every word carried the pill's padding, which made the gaps between words about twice a word space. A margin draws the padding back, so the pill reaches into the gaps instead of widening them (`PILL` in `widths.mjs`, which counts what is left; the browser weighs a word the same way, by its letters and that rest, not by its whole pill); phrases are cut by length.
- `wipe`: phrases are cut by length.
- `clean`: as it was.

These are changes on purpose to captions nobody placed, so the comparison in `tools/test.mjs` names the commit that made them as the version before.

Each of the eight was rendered on Sprites and looked at on a light talking head, the same darkened and washed out to near white, a busy bright screen recording, in 16:9 and 1:1, with a brand kit, with a card, and placed left-aligned in a narrow area (about 100 renders, 2026-11-17); sizes, edges, shadows and gaps were set against strips of the Shorts each is modelled on.

In the styles that set capitals (`hormozi`, `impact`, `wipe`) a few letters stay small, or they would say something else: the letter of a paragraph or a house number, when the word is a number with one small letter at its end ("§ 202a", "12b", "5m": not "202A", not five million), and a gender ending after its mark ("GENERALIST:innen", "LEHRER*in", "JEDE:r", "MITARBEITER/innen"). A capital I inside a word ("GeneralistInnen", "LehrerIn") is such a mark too, the only one capitals would swallow whole: it is set as the one small letter, "GENERALISTiNNEN" (before "nnen" always, before a single "n" at the word's end only after an ending a person's noun has, "LEHRERiN", "KOLLEGiN", "ÄRZTiN", so "LINKEDIN", "PLUGIN" and "LOGIN" stay whole; a form the list does not know, "NotarIn", stays "NOTARIN": write it "Notar:in"). Such a word is written into the built file in its capitals and left out of the style's transform, and the tool names it in a line (`in capitals but for small letters …`), so that whoever reads it can write a word otherwise. A digit inside a word ("B2B", "H2O", "3D-DRUCK"), units of several letters ("10KG", "5M/S") and everything else are set in capitals as before.

The fonts are among those HyperFrames embeds by itself when it renders (Montserrat, Poppins, Inter, League Gothic, Playfair Display, Oswald, Archivo Black, Outfit, …): no font files to ship, and the render on a clean machine looks like the preview (all twelve rendered on Sprites with nothing but HyperFrames 0.9.58 installed, 2026-11-17). With `--brand brand/DESIGN.md` (from brand-kit) the brand's display font (`typography.headline-display.fontFamily`) becomes the captions' font: one served by Google is linked, one given as files is copied beside the captions and declared with `@font-face`. `impact` keeps its own (the style is its narrow typeface), `editorial` its serif italic for the key words. The brand's `primary` colour goes where the style shows a colour (`look.brand`): to the accent in the four first styles, as ever (the word being said in `hormozi`, the pill in `karaoke`; `wipe` and `clean` show none); to the word being said and the key words alike in `fill`; to the key words in the styles where no word lights up (`bold`, `outline`, `single`, `editorial`, `subtitle`, `label`) and to the captions in colour in `impact`. Key words stay on the picture, so they must be read there: a primary under 4.5 : 1 against the dark edge on a picture (a dark blue), under 3 : 1 against the white box of a `label` line (a yellow), or one that is neither a hex colour nor `rgb()`, is left out (in `fill` for the word being said as well), the style keeps its own colours, and the tool says so in a line.

**`--follow`** (2026-11-19, for narrated explainers): in a style whose words stay as they are (`clean`, `bold`, `subtitle`, `editorial`, `outline`, `impact`) or only turn the text's colour (`label`), the word being said lights up: `active: "fill"` with the words waiting at half their colour (`label` keeps its grey), the light in the brand's primary where the style gives the brand the word being said or the key words and it reads there, else the style's key colour. Styles that light the word already, and `single`, stay as they are and the tool says so. Without `--follow` every built file is as before.

**`--edge`** (2026-11-19, for a ground as light as the box): a style's box gets an edge of the other lightness, as thick as a card's border (3 px at 58 px type): `label`'s white box of every line a dark ring (`rgba(17,24,39,0.30)`) and a soft shadow, `clean`'s dark box a light ring. Made because `label`'s boxes vanished on a narrated explainer's near-white ground (part one of the word-timing films: the words read, the box did not); `label --follow --edge` on that ground was rendered and looked at the same day. Styles without a box stay as they are and the tool says so; without `--edge` every built file is as before.

### Groups and timing (`tools/lib/group.mjs`)

- First the phrases: a group never runs over the end of a sentence, a pause (0.25–0.45 s by style) or a comma once it is half full.
- A phrase too long for one group is split into groups of about equal size: "Let me show | how it works.", not "Let me show how | it works."; never past the style's words or characters.
- The styles of up to five words split it by length instead of by equal counts of words (`even` in the style's group): the fewest groups that keep to the words and characters, and of those the most alike in length. Equal counts take a group more whenever a pair of long words passes the characters, and then leave words alone ("Wir | haben | letzte | Woche" where "Wir haben letzte | Woche vierundzwanzig" holds). Counted on four real takes and two spoken texts (about 190 captions of two or three words a style): single words that need not stand alone 19 → 9 in `bold`, captions on screen for under 0.4 s 17 → 4 (`hormozi` 17 → 6, `karaoke` 4 → 0, `wipe` 6 → 1). The two styles that set whole phrases, `clean` and `subtitle`, gain nothing by it (no word alone either way, and a cut by length ends a line on "und" as readily) and keep the equal counts.
- A group appears when its first word is said and stays 0.35 s after its last, at least the style's minimum (0.5–1 s) when there is room, never into the next group (20 ms apart). Only one group is ever on screen.
- The word being said is active from its start to the next word's start, so a short pause inside a group leaves no gap.

### Placement (agentitos' [platform-zones](../platform-zones/))

- The captions keep clear of what the apps lay over the video: `build` and `project` ask platform-zones for the frame (`tools/lib/zones.mjs`: `PLATFORM_ZONES`, else `../platform-zones` beside this skill, else `/home/sprite/skills/platform-zones`) and set the captions in its centred box (the safe box even about the frame's middle): their lowest line at its bottom, centred on the frame, never wider, unless the caller names another place (below). This skill keeps no numbers of its own.
- `--platform reels,tiktok` names where the video goes (`node ../platform-zones/tools/zones.mjs list` names them all). Without it, the usual platforms of the frame together: in 9:16 Reels, Stories, TikTok, Shorts and Facebook Reels, the lowest line at 1440 px of 1920, centred, at most 696 px wide (840 for Reels alone), clear of the buttons' column on the right; in 4:5 and 1:1 the feeds; in 16:9 YouTube and the feeds.
- Each build says for which platforms it placed them.
- The styles' sizes are for a frame whose short side is 1080 px and scale with the short side, so a 1920×1080 frame gets the same type as a 1080×1920 one, a 3840×2160 frame twice as big.
- `project --size 1080x1920` puts a wide video into a vertical frame (and the other way round): `--fit cover` (the default) fills the frame and cuts the video at its edges, `--fit contain` fits it in with bars; the captions are placed for the frame, not for the video.
- Each group is set at the style's size and shrinks (to 55 % at most) until it fits its lines and no word is wider than its room. The words are inline blocks between spaces with `text-wrap: balance`, so the lines balance and no word is left alone on the second.
- A word that is wider than its room even then ("VERWALTUNGSWISSENSCHAFTLER*innen" in `hormozi`) is set as small as it takes, alone or with the words of its group; before, it was cut off at the frame's edge. Whoever reads the words can do better than small: a soft hyphen (U+00AD, `\u00ad` in the JSON) in a word's text lets it break there, with a hyphen, into two lines at a larger size ("Verwaltungs-" over "wissenschaftler*innen"). Where a word may break is a judgement, so the tool never hyphenates by itself; the subtitle file leaves the soft hyphens out.

### Another place, named by the caller

The bottom of the safe box is wrong for some pictures: in a close selfie it is the speaker's chin and the free picture is the wall above the head; in a 16:9 frame with the speaker as a tall panel on the left the captions belong in the free area on the right, left-aligned, sometimes much larger. The tool cannot see that and does not try: it places the captions, whoever looked at the frames (an agent, a layout tool) decides where. `build` and `project` take the place in px of the frame, each option on its own; what is not given stays as above, and without any of them the built file is byte for byte what it was before there were options (`tools/test.mjs` builds both and compares).

| option | what it sets | when a caller needs it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--y <px>` | the y, from the frame's top, at which the lowest line ends (a style's box ends there, its padding included) | the safe box's bottom lies on what must be seen: a chin, a product, a screen's lower edge |
| `--x <px>` | where the captions' area starts from the left; without it the area is centred on the frame | the free area lies at one side |
| `--max-width <px>` | how wide the area is; without it the centred box's width, or with `--x` up to the centred box's right edge | the free area is narrower or wider than the centred box |
| `--align left\|centre\|right` | the lines in the area (default centre); left or right, a style's box and a card stand at that edge and end where their longest line ends, and a group pops in from that edge | the area begins beside something (a panel, a list): every group starts at the same edge |
| `--font-size <px>` | the type's size in px of the frame instead of the style's (not `--size`, the frame's); the stroke and a card's border grow with it | the area is much larger or smaller than the strip the styles are made for |
| `--lines 1\|2` | how many lines a group may have (default 2) | the area is low: a strip of wall above a head, a bar under a screen |

- **The safe box is said, not enforced.** The tool answers where it put the captions (`Placed where asked: the lowest line ends at y 440, centred in x 192–888 (at most 696 px wide), one line of 60 px type.`) and asks platform-zones' own `check` about that area, as high as its lines at that size with the padding of a style's box or a card around them: inside the safe box, or the stretch above the buttons, of the platforms it names, or one line beginning with `!`: how far past which edge, what the apps lay there, and how far it passes the frame when it does (`--x 900 --max-width 400` in a frame 1080 wide). The file is built all the same; captions under an app's buttons are the commonest mistake, but the place is the caller's.
- **The area holds a style's box whole.** A box (`clean`) or a card counts with its padding, whatever `box-sizing` the host composition sets, and the padding is no room for a word: left- or right-aligned the box begins and ends inside `--x` and `--max-width`. (Captions nobody placed keep the box they had: in a host that sets no `box-sizing` its padding comes on top of the centred box's width.)
- **The type keeps its size.** A style's groups are made for two lines of its size in the centred box and lean on the shrinking above; in one line, a narrow area or larger type they would shrink to the least and still break into another line. So in a width, in lines or at a size of the caller's own, a group must set in its lines at the type's size (the one given with `--font-size`, else the style's), line by line as a browser fills them. The words are measured, not estimated: `tools/lib/widths.mjs` holds every character's width in the styles' fonts (measured in Chrome with Google's font files; kerning only draws characters together, so what fits by them fits), with the style's case, letter spacing, gaps and pills. A phrase whose groups set keeps them; any other is cut anew (`tools/lib/group.mjs`): the fewest groups that set, about equally long ("wie ihr eure | Instagram-Storys | für die ganze | Woche vorplant." in one 696 px line of 60 px type). Captions that are only moved or aligned (`--y`, `--align`), or given the centred box's width and two lines, keep the style's groups.
- **What cannot keep it is said.** A word wider than a line of the area at that size stands alone and is set smaller (one with a hyphen may break at it instead); the tool names these words (`Wider than a line of this area at 110 px, …: "Storrito,", "browser".`), so the caller can give more width or a smaller size. A single short word beside it that would be on screen for less than the style's minimum ("Am" for 0.15 s before "Montagmorgen") would only flash by: where the area has two lines it stays with the wide word, on a line of its own, set smaller with it, and the tool names it too.
- **Key words in a typeface of their own are measured in it.** `editorial` sets its key words in Playfair Display italic at 1.24 of the size, wider than the sans around them ("Montagmorgen": 8.3 em instead of 7.3); `widths.mjs` has that typeface too, so a group with a key word sets in its lines as the browser sets it and is not shrunk. A card (`box`) is what the person chose to stand together: it is never cut to an area, shrinks to the lines asked for and takes another line where it cannot, and the tool says so when there is one. With a brand's font the words are measured in the style's own: nearly right for a display sans, and the shrinking takes the rest.
- Seen on stills of two real cases (2026-11-17): a close selfie in 9:16 with `--style clean --y 440 --max-width 696 --font-size 60 --lines 1` (one line on the wall above the head, every group at 60 px), and a 16:9 frame with the speaker as a panel on the left with `--style clean --x 704 --y 930 --max-width 1120 --align left`. Laid out in Chrome the same day: 3,147 groups from 36 builds (the four styles in nine areas, German and English sentences), in a host with and without `box-sizing: border-box`: every group of several words at the size asked for and in its lines, every box inside its area; `tools/test.mjs` repeats a few of them when the machine has a Chrome.

### The sub-composition (`tools/lib/render.mjs`)

- `build` writes `<folder>/index.html` and prints the clip to put into the host composition: `<div id="captions" data-composition-id="captions" data-composition-src="captions/index.html" data-start="0" data-duration="…" data-track-index="9" data-track-kind="captions" data-width="…" data-height="…">`.
- Beside it `<folder>/area.json` says where they stand, in px of the frame, as high as their lines can be: `{"left", "width", "top", "bottom", "size", "lines"}`. agentitos' b-roll reads it to lay its beats beside the person out around the captions, and moves the mount onto the band it keeps for them while such a beat shows.
- The file follows the sub-composition rules of the HyperFrames core skill: everything the runtime needs (style, root, script) inside the `<template>`, the root styled by `#root`, the host slot, the root and the timeline all with the id `captions`.
- The groups, words and times are written into the file. The script builds the words, waits for the fonts (`document.fonts.ready`), fits each group, then registers one paused GSAP timeline. Deterministic: no random, no clock.
- Every group is set hidden at its end (`tl.set`), whatever its exit; an exit never starts before the last word is fully in and said.
- `project` writes a whole host composition, the video as one clip with its sound and the captions mounted: what the captions agent shows and renders.

## Subtitle files (`tools/lib/srt.mjs`)

`srt` writes cues to the usual rules of readable subtitles: at most two lines of 42 characters broken near the middle, a new cue at the end of a sentence or a pause of 0.6 s, at least 0.8 s and at most 6 s on screen, long enough to read at 17 characters a second where the next cue leaves room.

## What it does not do (yet)

- Choose where the captions go: it knows what the apps cover, not what the picture shows.
- Captions that start at a top edge or stand in the middle of an area's height, and more than two lines: they always end on a y and grow upwards.
- Keep a word with a hyphen in one piece: one that is wider than its area ("Instagram-Storys" in very large type) breaks at the hyphen instead of shrinking.
- Measure a brand's font: placed captions in one are cut by the widths of the style's own font.
