# clips

One long video in, finished vertical shorts out, **in the video's own look**: the speaker's footage cut to the moment, framed 9:16 on whoever speaks, pauses and "äh"s cut, every clip at the same level, captions, and a hook card where the first line does not hook. The moments are whole passages that **stand alone** for the channel's followers: from the question or the first sentence to the last conclusion, nothing that leans on the rest of the video, 59 seconds by default, longer only when the person wants longer clips (a trade: short passages then come back with more around them).

> Agents: start with [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). It is written for [rragent](https://github.com/storrito/rragent): the previews are a project of its `hyperframes` template, the shorts are published with agentitos' [publish-video](../publish-video/) through Storrito.

```bash
node tools/episode.mjs fetch https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=… work/episode   # every word, its time and speaker
node tools/episode.mjs names work/episode --take 4                          # fetch ran it (fetch --no-names skips it): take a proposed name (--leave one applied, --also "Name")
node tools/episode.mjs moments work/episode                                 # what is inside, the moments, the ideas to record anew (--max-seconds 100: longer clips, a trade)
node tools/clips.mjs prepare work/episode work/01 412-431 \
  --out artifacts/clips/composition/01-posting --platform reels,tiktok,shorts --hook "Daily posting *hurt* us" \
  --drop 418-420 --payoff 429                                                 # leave the marked sentences out; the strongest line's words
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs build work/01/takes                        # the preview
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs captions work/01/takes --style hormozi
node tools/clips.mjs place work/01                                            # the text off the faces (prepare ran it once; it builds again)
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs check work/01/takes
node tools/clips.mjs render work/01 work/out/01.mp4                          # the video at −14 LUFS, and 01.srt
```

Requirements: Node 20+, ffmpeg, the HyperFrames CLI (for the render), OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, or on a Sprite the gateway's `openrouter` connection), and agentitos' [best-takes](../best-takes/), [captions](../captions/), [layout](../layout/), [platform-zones](../platform-zones/) and [transcribe](../transcribe/) beside this skill (for b-roll also [b-roll](../b-roll/)) (transcribe also through `TRANSCRIBE` naming its `tools/transcribe.mjs`). yt-dlp from the PATH, else downloaded once into `~/.cache/agentitos/`. No npm dependencies.

## How it works

1. **`episode.mjs fetch`** reads a link's facts with yt-dlp, takes its sound and writes down every word with its time and speaker (ElevenLabs Scribe through OpenRouter, by agentitos' transcribe skill with `--speakers`: pieces side by side, and once more in long requests where their voices cannot be told to be the same people; about 22 cents an hour, twice that with the long requests; the noises it marks are left out); without OpenRouter it takes YouTube's own captions, without speakers. A file goes the same way, its sound written down as it is. `sentences.txt` numbers the sentences (they end at . ! ? and where the voice changes).
2. **`episode.mjs names`** (fetch ends with it) spells the names in the words as the video's page spells them. The speech model writes what it hears: on the test episodes "Reed" for Reade (Gonzo Girl's Walker Reade), "Werewolf" for Robert Eggers' Werwulf, "Andoran Tubik oder Kanaki" for the licence issuers Anjouan, Tobique and Kahnawake, "aus'm Alter" for "aus Malta". `fetch` keeps the page in `page.json`: title, channel, the whole description without its links, the chapters, the tags, the creators, and what the video's own uploaded captions (never YouTube's automatic ones) write that the transcription never does. One call to Gemini 3.8 Flash with little thinking reads the page and the numbered sentences and lists the page's names and every place it finds a name misspelt, with the right spelling and how sure it is (1.2 cents for 23 minutes, 2 cents for 53; with the model's default thinking it was 6.6 cents and found less). The tool then decides by rules it can count, never by the model alone:
   - **refused** (never applied): the words are not in the sentence as written; the correction leaves out or adds a word, takes off or puts on an ending ("Arquette's" → "Arquette"), does not sound like what is written (`sounds`, a rough sound key of the spelling: below 0.5), or has more or fewer sounds; two voices say the words; the page writes the written name so already; neither side is a name. Words at either end that stay the same are no part of a correction, and a run of names is split word by word;
   - **applied**: the page writes the right spelling (also with an ending: "Werwulf's"), it sounds like what is written (0.75 or more: Reed and Reade 0.75, Werewolf and Werwulf 0.86), the written words are no ordinary word of this transcript (none in lower case, and a single word the transcript never writes in lower case elsewhere: the "werewolf" of a creature keeps the film's title from being applied), more than case or spacing changes, and the model is sure;
   - **proposed**: everything else, with why: the model's knowledge (a name the page does not write), sounds less alike, an ordinary word, only the case, the model not sure. The agent takes one with `--take`.

   A correction holds every place the same words are written the same way. It changes the words' text and keeps their times: two words made one take both their times, a word more shares the last one's; each changed word keeps what was written (`was`) and its correction's number (`name`). `words.raw.json` keeps the words as written; `words.json`, `sentences.json` and `sentences.txt` are written from it with the applied corrections, so `--take` and `--leave` change them without a model and a run again asks the model anew (what was taken stays taken). `names.json` holds the page's names, the list with every place, the model's own answer (`found`) and its cost. `fetch --no-names` leaves the call out (the words keep the speech model's spellings; `names` can run later); where YouTube gives no facts of the video, the sound still comes and the names have no page, so none is applied by itself. A word the transcript writes in lower case is applied where the page writes the name with the same two words around it ("Robert Eggers' werewolf" against the description's "Robert Eggers' Werwulf"), at those places only. `prepare` puts the applied ones onto each clip's own words (best-takes writes them anew) as `cut.json` `"fixes"`, by the time of each place, where the clip's word sounds like the name, and names the places where the clip heard something else; after a `--take` or `--leave`, `clips.mjs names <clip>` does it again (what the list put there is kept in `takes/names.json`, so its old fixes go and the agent's own stay), then build again: the captions, the `.srt` and the hook's words carry them; best-takes and captions are not changed for it. The transcription is not given the names beforehand: OpenRouter's speech endpoint takes `keyterms`, but they cost extra, bias every word towards the list, and best-takes writes every clip's words anew, which would need them too.

   Measured on two episodes (2026-11-19, the words of main's fetch, two runs each): Hot Ones with Willem Dafoe (23 min, 43 names, 75 places; 3 misspelt, 11 more in the wrong case): the 3 applied both runs, nothing else applied; proposed the sauce Da'Bomb as the channel's captions write it (7 places, case and apostrophe) and one ordinary word. Finanzfluss with Lilith Wittmann (53 min, 57 names, 258 places, 8 misspelt: the transcription now writes Curaçao, Malta, Glücksspiel, Wittmann, Tipico, Merkur, Schufa and ID-Wallet right): nothing applied, as none of the eight is a spelling the page writes; proposed "aus Malta" both runs, the three licence islands, "Platin Casino" for "Stakelogic Casino" in one run, and "Kallmann" for the transcript's Keilmann and Kalmann (not to be taken: the page does not say which is right). Fetched twice anew on the live link (the transcription differs a little from run to run): 3 applied each time (Reade, Werwulf's, and Werwulf where the transcript wrote "Werewolf"; once it wrote "werewolf", which the same two words before it on the page let through; once also "Jiang" → "Jang", the sauce as the channel's captions write it), 1.1 and 1.4 cents. The robe passage prepared from it, built and rendered: "Walker Reade," in the captions and the .srt; with the correction left out (`--leave`, `clips.mjs names`, build) the two compositions differ only in that word's text, its times, group and size the same, the rest of the rendered video the same (SSIM 0.9998).
3. **`episode.mjs moments`** has Gemini 3.8 Flash read `sentences.txt` once (5 to 9 cents an hour), so that the agent's chat does not carry the whole video through every later step. It started from [Melvynx' `/clips`](https://github.com/Melvynx/video-agent-skills) (MIT) and was changed after a measurement against the shorts that five channels' own editors cut from nine episodes (November 2026: the old rubric gave every moment 8 to 10 points of 10 and returned only part of every passage longer than 59 s):
   - what the video holds: claims, stories, numbers, mistakes, how-tos, answers, exchanges (a quick back-and-forth with a laugh). Under four in all it is too thin for clips;
   - each moment a **whole passage**, a range of whole sentences: an answer from its question (when the first sentence answers a question just before it, the tool notes that question in `checks.asked` and moves nothing: a rule that moved the start there took it away from the editors' start every time it touched a covered pick), a story from its first sentence, to its last conclusion, not the first finished thought. `--max-seconds` (default 59, it fits every post type Storrito offers) is how long a short may be, and a higher one is a trade, not an improvement: at 100 s long passages come back whole more often and short ones with more around them ("Measured"); a passage up to 1.7 times that is returned whole with the sentences an editor would cut marked (`drop`), never its first or last sentence or its payoff;
   - the sentence with the strongest line (`payoff`), and a hook for the screen (6 words or fewer, never a label) where the opening does not hook;
   - "stands alone" for the channel's own followers: the video's title and description go into the prompt, the names in them count as introduced;
   - the moments **in the order of the episode**, with `opens`, the model's 1–10 for how well the first sentence works on someone who has seen nothing (it decides which of two overlapping moments stays). No order is claimed: the old score, the model's own ranking and `opens` put the editors' picks ahead of the other moments in 0.66, 0.45–0.56 and 0.50 of the pairs on the nine tuning episodes, and the episode's order in 0.34–0.45 on three others (0.5 is chance; see "Measured"). What can be counted the tool counts itself (`checks`): whether it opens with a question, the question just before it that it answers (`asked`), a conjunction in the middle of a voice's turn, how many voices, seconds over the limit;
   - the ideas too spread out over the video to cut, worth recording anew.

   `moments.json`: `{ limit, count, inventory, moments: [{ first, last, kind, title, why, opens, payoff, hook, crosstalk, drop, start, end, seconds, kept, checks: { question, conjunction, voices, over, asked }, opening, link }], ideas }`, in the order of the episode. `kept` is the length without the `drop` sentences.

4. **`episode.mjs cut`** downloads only the stretch (up to 4K: a 9:16 window of a wide shot is a third of its width) with a breath of air around it, or cuts it from the file.
5. **`frame.mjs`** cuts footage wider than the frame to it, on whoever speaks. The shots are the footage's own, to the frame: `lib/cuts.mjs` compares every frame at its own time over one frame up to 2.2 seconds, so a hard cut (also a very dark one), a talk show's push wipe of a quarter second, a dissolve and a fade through black are one cut each, measured against the picture's own motion around it (ffmpeg's scene score found 4 of 10 cuts on Hot Ones' dark set). A change of only part of the picture (someone standing up), one whose picture comes back within a second (a hand before the lens) and a small one count only where the model names a cut; a flash inside one shot is none, a flash into another picture one; no shot is shorter than six frames. Gemini 3.8 Flash then watches the clip once (a small copy without the file's tags) with stills of every shot (its first, middle and last frame, one every three seconds between; three to seven cents a clip) and gives each shot's kind and, on every still, every face with the voice it speaks with, its box (ear to ear), a box each of its eyes, its nose and its mouth, and where it looks (left, right, the camera; asked for both eyes, the far one of a three-quarter view too: on twenty stills of the guest's close-ups the boxes were right on all and the direction on all; a face called "camera" with its features to one side of its box leans a little that way). The three are asked for one by one and checked, because one box of all three ended at the nose on five of eight close-up stills of a guest with glasses (the mouth line 120 px too high, the nose line on the glasses): the eyes above the nose above the mouth, and from the eyes' top to the mouth's bottom at least 38 % of the head's box (0.40–0.67 on the stills whose boxes were right, 0.29–0.37 on those of the guest); a still whose features fail, or jump against the stills beside it (their middle a fifth of the face away from where those two, which agree, lead, while the face is said to look the same way: one wrong box at a close-up's middle swung the window by 30 % of its width for four seconds, and the edge check, using the same box, said nothing), is left out, the stills beside it hold, and the list names it with ⚠; where no still of a face holds, the head's proportions stand in (the mouth's bottom at 86 % of the box, the low end of what was measured, so that a mouth line from them is never above the real one). An answer of before (one `features_2d` for all three) is checked by that share alone. Where a close-up's head moves or tips between two stills (its followed box leaves the straight line between them by more than a tenth of its size), a still more is asked for at the moment furthest off the line, three a shot at most, in a second request of images only (robe: five stills, under a cent; the mouth placed 0.4 of the features' height too high while the head tipped down there, and captions on it 1.5 s, before; 0 s after, by the review's measure). The window sits on the face of the voice heard most, a third from the top, a turn under 1.5 s (a "ja", a laugh) moves nothing, a reaction shot shows who it shows; between the stills the face is followed in the frames (`lib/track.mjs`: ten a second, forward from one still and back from the next, no model; a still the frames from both sides do not lead to is the model's slip, left out and listed; two faces crossing go straight from still to still; a face missing on a still is out of the picture, not cut), and the window holds it all through: still where it can, wider where that is enough, else following it lazily (it rests while the face keeps 3 % inside, goes along when it pushes, and never turns back within a second unless the face itself would leave). Of a close-up (a face wider than half the window) the window holds the eyes, nose and mouth, never closer to the edge than 6 % of the window's width (best-takes' punch-in takes 5.4 % of each side), and keeps three quarters of the head's width in the picture where the features allow; room on the side the face looks to (18 % of the face's width more than behind, the window's middle on that side of the features') takes what the head leaves, at most half of the window's play; the back of the head, an ear, hair at the side may be cut, as the channel's editors and OpusClip cut them. Between two stills the features keep their place in the head's box, which the tracker follows; where the head turns between them they go from the one place to the other. The room is taken as it is on average over two seconds, so a swaying head moves nothing; only the features about to come closer to the edge than those 6 % move the window at once. The numbers were chosen on the five passages of our three-way test (with more room a swaying close-up left the window too little play, 0.65 turns a second; with less, the far eye of a three-quarter view and a pair of glasses came to the edge in the rendered clips; without the head's share half the frame stood empty in front of a face at a sway's end, with 80 % the window followed the sway again). An answer without the features (a `clip.people.json` from before) falls back to the middle 80 % of the face's box (`FRAME_CORE=0.9` before `clips.mjs reframe` or `frame.mjs` changes that share), which in a three-quarter view lets the nose or an eye go. A face low in a wide shot (an empty wall above it) gets a closer window, down to 55 % of the footage's height; a wide shot of the room in which someone of the talk speaks is cut to them even when Gemini calls it B-roll or split, as long as Gemini says the picture shows the people, not material (whole, their face would be 2 to 5 % of the frame's height; the channels' editors and OpusClip cut to the speaker there). Slides, a screen or a product with the speaker small beside them stay whole; where Gemini does not say, the shot stays whole and the list says how to cut it to the speaker. The tool checks its own windows on every still and at each piece's first, middle and last frame and names, with a ⚠, every face whose eyes, nose or mouth are cut by an edge or closer to it than 1.5 % of the width (a small face: the whole face, with 2 %); only an ear, a cheek or hair at the edge of a close-up is listed without a ⚠, as accepted; a face half out of the footage itself is not the window's. Each close-up says how calm its window is (turns a second) and how much of its time the features are in with their room (`closeup` of the piece in `framed.json`). What a window would cut apart is shown whole, above the captions over a blurred, darker copy of itself: B-roll (a photo, archive footage, a place, a document, a screen; Gemini names each shot's kind and the box that must stay visible) and a picture split into feeds side by side. Two who speak, too far apart for one window, go one above the other (two panels from the top, the captions on the lower one's chest): a split screen of two voices always, one shot of both while they talk back and forth (three turns or more of under five seconds). Without OpenRouter the footage stays centred. `framed.mp4` keeps the footage's timing (a picture that starts a frame after its sound holds its first frame) and a keyframe every second for the preview's seeks. The shots (each person's track, the stills with `parts`: the features as shares of the face's box and `lean`), the windows (a close-up's with `closeup`: `turns`, `held`, `inside`) and the faces at an edge (`part`: `features`, a fault, or `side`, accepted) are kept in `framed.json`; `framed.sheet.jpg` (eight shots a sheet, drawn cell by cell so that a Sprite's memory holds it; a cell without a frame is grey and named) shows per shot the footage with the window drawn in and the frame at its first, middle and last frame, red where eyes, nose or mouth are at an edge; `clips.mjs reframe <clip> --whole <s>` / `--follow <s>` (one window on who speaks, never two above each other) changes a shot's kind without asking the model again and keeps the edit.
6. **`clips.mjs prepare`** runs the three above for one moment and hands the framed clip to [best-takes](../best-takes/) (`takes.mjs new --aspect 9:16 --pauses tight --punch on`): ElevenLabs Scribe writes the clip's words again, word-exact, "äh"s included; every pause over 0.4 s goes whatever the picture shows; at every jump cut the picture goes 12 % closer or back out, closer on a key word, never on a piece shown whole or two above each other (`takes/source/still.json`); one keep of the whole moment goes into `takes/cut.json`, from the first word heard whole to the last (the cut's air may hold the tail of the sentence before). `--drop 418-420` leaves those sentences out: a keep on each side, a jump cut between. `--payoff 429` prints that sentence's words in the clip as a keep (with `"again": true`, so that best-takes plays it where it was said too): put it at the top of `cut.json` to open the short with the strongest line spoken (the keeps play in their order); none for a line that is the moment's first sentence, takes over 10 s, or would bring the short over the limit. The limit is `moments.json`'s (`--max-seconds` for another): `prepare` says when the moment without its dropped sentences is still over it and hands it to best-takes as `--target`, so that `check` warns while the cut is longer. From there the edit is best-takes': `build` (with `--hook` for the card), `captions`, `check`.
7. **`clips.mjs place`** (prepare runs it once; again after the cut is built, as it builds again) puts the text where the faces are not, by agentitos' [layout](../layout/) (`place.mjs text`) from every face of every shot as the framing tracked it, in the frame (`lib/faces.mjs`: through each window, shown whole, as two panels):
   - **one place for the captions of the whole clip** (published Shorts keep theirs in one place within a video, their middle at 60–75 % of the height, one line of about three words, the type 4 % of the height): every height of the safe box at the usual type (77 px) and at 90 and 80 % of it (69, 62 px; never under the floor of 61 px), the one with the least on a face, then the least raised, then the usual place and size; one line for a style of a few words, the style's two for one of whole phrases (captions' `styles --json`; `--lines 1|2`); the area the built style's line with the ink of its outline and shadow below it, the captions' line and width inside it;
   - **each shot raised** that needs it, so that the chin and the margin below it clear the captions (`frame.mjs --lift`: the window lower in the footage where it reaches further, the rest over a blurred band at the bottom, under the apps' bars), once for the whole shot, by its lowest moment, every return of the same close-up at one height, by one of three levels: `--raise head` only while the whole head stays in the picture (the box's top and layout's margin for the hair below the frame's top; the type to its floor first, the chin or lower lip said), `--raise mouth` (the default) as head and past the head's top where the captions would lie on a mouth, never with the eyes above 27 % of the height (the upper end of the eye line in published Shorts), `--raise full` until the chin is clear, as far as the eyes reach the safe box's top; the list says per shot how far the frame cuts the head (px and seconds, and the raise's share);
   - **never on the mouth**: a face's lines from its eyes, nose and mouth as the framing knows them (the nose line 70 % down them, the lip line 88 %, the mouth line their bottom and 15 % of their height more, for an open mouth and a head that tips between two stills; the head's proportions where the model's boxes did not hold); the type smaller first, then the raise past the head; on the lower lip or the chin at the floor said as not avoidable at a readable size; a `✗` (the mouth, the nose or the eyes, a group below the floor) only where a cut, a smaller type by hand, a lift or a soft hyphen can still avoid it;
   - **the hook** (layout's two kinds) where it clears every face over its seconds and the captions: a short text below the chin or above the head, else a card at the top; a card in the text's place where the picture behind it is not a close-up (hands, things, a photo), also where no place is free; it ends at a cut when the shot after leaves it no place;
   - what still lies on a face, per shot, the part with its seconds, with what would avoid it; a place of its own for a shot only proposed (two seconds on a face saved at least), never taken.

   It writes `takes/place.json` (the captions' options for agentitos' captions, the hook's kind and box), `takes/source/faces.json` (the faces in the frame, for best-takes' punch-ins, which grow away from the captions, and for its check, which names text on a face), `placement/text.json` (layout's whole answer) and `placed.sheet.jpg` (per shot its first, middle and last frame with the captions yellow, the hook cyan, the faces red). `--caption-y`, `--font-size`, `--lines 1|2`, `--lift <s>:<px>` and `--hook text|card` fix one choice and are kept (a `--caption-y` below the safe box is refused); what `reframe` and `place` asked of the framing is kept in `reframe.json`, so that one does not undo the other. [`references/text.md`](references/text.md) says what it decides and what is left.

   Measured on five passages of two channels (2026-11-18, the framing with three feature boxes and a still more where a close-up's head moves; an independent measure: the captions' ink from Chrome against the eyes, nose, mouth and chin a model found on the rendered frames, every half second): captions on the nose or eyes 0 s everywhere, the hook on a face 0 s, ink outside the safe box 9.5–17.5 s a clip before the review, 0 since. On the mouth, robe / ice-bowl / deaths: 1.5 / 0 / 0.5 s before the review; `--raise mouth` with the eyes up to the apps' top bar 0 / 0 / 0 (the head cut up to 295, 171 and 330 px, the eyes up to 12 % of the height); `--raise mouth` now, the eyes no higher than 27 %: 1.5 / 0 / 0.5 (the type 62, 62 and 69 px, one close-up of deaths raised 112 px); `--raise head`: 1.5 / – / 0 (62 px, no head cut further). The German podcast clips: 0 at every level, 77 px in two lines, long words broken at soft hyphens (one word of three parts 47 px, named).
8. **`clips.mjs render`** (`render-all <clips.json> <folder>` for all, one after the other; `prepare-all <episode> <clips.json>` prepares them side by side, as many as the memory holds) renders the built preview with HyperFrames (6 Mbit/s) and runs best-takes' `finish`: −14 LUFS, a true-peak limit, the subtitles as `.srt` beside the video.

`kinetic-lyrics` uses the same `episode.mjs` and `frame.mjs` (its `tools/` run them from here) and puts every word big on screen instead.

## Measured (November 2026)

The moments were held against the shorts that channels' own editors cut from the same episodes: nine episodes of five channels to tune on (27 editors' shorts that are one passage of the episode, 15 of them longer than 59 s), then once three episodes of three other channels (12 shorts, 3 longer than 59 s). Three runs each, means; chance is the same number of spans of the same lengths at random places. "Order" is the share of pairs in which a moment that covers an editor's short is listed before one that covers none (0.5 is chance).

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nine, before | 9.3 of 27 (2.5) | 0 of 15 | 4.3 of 12 | 5.7 / 6.7 | 4.3 | 0.66 (score) | 38 s |
| nine, now, 59 s | 10.3 (4.1) | 1.3 | 5.7 | 7.7 / 6.3 | 6.3 | 0.55 (episode) | 52 s |

("Near now, 100 s" is the version just before `opens`; the selection prompt is otherwise the same. "Final" is this version, with the mechanics that cut a dropped sentence by its words and hold the limit, measured on 2026-11-18; a prompt that asked the model for its list in the episode's order instead of best first did worse: at 59 s 8.7 covered, 4.7 short ones whole, 0.3 long ones whole; at 100 s 11.0, 3.0 and 4.7.) What holds on both sets: the moments are whole passages now, longer, and more of the editors' short passages come back whole (nine: 5.7 of 12 instead of 4.3; three: 5.3 of 9 instead of 3.3). What does not: the gain in covered shorts is no larger than the gain of chance from the longer moments, starts and ends are not closer to the editors' on the three, long passages come back whole only on the nine and only at 100 s, and no order of the moments (the old score, the model's ranking, `opens`, the counted checks, the episode's order) puts the editors' shorts ahead reliably. A second, local pass for the edges (one small call per moment with the two minutes around it) did not help on either set (three: starts within 2 s 1.3 instead of 2.0) and cost 11–14 cents an hour more; it was taken out. Small numbers: about ten covered shorts a run on the nine, five or six on the three.

## What is next

Divide the whole episode into its passages first (from the question that opens a topic to its last beat, a topic line each), then let the agent and the person choose among them. Tried once as an experiment, three runs: on the nine episodes both edges lay within 5 s of the editors' for 10 of 27 shorts (moments: about 6; chance 0.2), 27–30 passages an hour, median 80–95 s, 3–4 cents an hour; on the three it was 0.7 of 12 (moments: 1.3), with passages of a median 120–155 s. It finds edges, not passages the selection misses, and only where its passages are as fine as the editors' cuts.

## Limits

- **Names off the page are proposed, not applied.** Where the page does not write a name (an island, a person only said), the right spelling is the model's knowledge, and the agent decides; a name the speech model wrote as an ordinary word that sounds alike ("Alter") is never applied either. What the transcript itself writes two ways (Keilmann, Kalmann) the tool cannot settle.
- A clip is only as good as its moment: a video full of "as we saw", "this slide" gives few clips and more ideas to record anew.
- The framing follows faces and voices and shows B-roll, photos and split screens whole, smaller, above the captions; a picture in a picture is cut as one picture.
- A subject that moves without speaking (a car, a player, an animal) is followed by [auto-reframe](../auto-reframe/) (CPU tracking, offline), not by this framing, which goes by faces and voices.
- **A mouth between two stills is not seen.** The eyes, nose and mouth are the model's on the stills (a few a shot, up to three more where a close-up's head moves); between two stills they ride on a straight line with the head's box, so a mouth that opens wide or a head that tips down there is an estimate: `place` may call captions on an open mouth "the lower lip". On the five passages (2026-11-18, the independent measure every half second) captions lay on a mouth for 1.5 s in one clip (robe, at 22.25, 22.75 and 26.25 s of the short, which the list called the lower lip) and 0.5 s in another (deaths), none in the other three. The list says which seconds on a lower lip come from such an estimate (more than 0.25 s from a still, `FEAT_EST` in `frame.mjs`), with the two stills: look at those seconds.
- Only material the person may use.
