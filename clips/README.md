# clips

One long video in, finished vertical shorts out, **in the video's own look**: the speaker's footage cut to the moment, framed 9:16 on whoever speaks, pauses and "äh"s cut, every clip at the same level, captions, and a hook card where the first line does not hook. The moments are the ones that **stand alone**: one idea, a cold open, a clean end, nothing that leans on the rest of the video, 20 to 59 seconds.

> Agents: start with [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). It is written for [rragent](https://github.com/storrito/rragent): the previews are a project of its `hyperframes` template, the shorts are published with agentitos' [publish-video](../publish-video/) through Storrito.

```bash
node tools/episode.mjs fetch https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=… work/episode   # every word, its time and speaker
node tools/episode.mjs moments work/episode                                   # what is inside, the moments, the ideas to record anew
node tools/clips.mjs prepare work/episode work/01 412-431 \
  --out artifacts/clips/composition/01-posting --platform reels,tiktok,shorts --hook "Daily posting *hurt* us"
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs build work/01/takes                        # the preview
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs captions work/01/takes --style hormozi
node tools/clips.mjs place work/01                                            # the text off the faces (prepare ran it once; it builds again)
node ../best-takes/tools/takes.mjs check work/01/takes
node tools/clips.mjs render work/01 work/out/01.mp4                          # the video at −14 LUFS, and 01.srt
```

Requirements: Node 20+, ffmpeg, the HyperFrames CLI (for the render), OpenRouter (`OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, or on a Sprite the gateway's `openrouter` connection), and agentitos' [best-takes](../best-takes/), [captions](../captions/), [layout](../layout/), [platform-zones](../platform-zones/) and [transcribe](../transcribe/) beside this skill (for b-roll also [b-roll](../b-roll/)) (transcribe also through `TRANSCRIBE` naming its `tools/transcribe.mjs`). yt-dlp from the PATH, else downloaded once into `~/.cache/agentitos/`. No npm dependencies.

## How it works

1. **`episode.mjs fetch`** reads a link's facts with yt-dlp, takes its sound and writes down every word with its time and speaker (ElevenLabs Scribe through OpenRouter, by agentitos' transcribe skill with `--speakers`: pieces side by side, and once more in long requests where their voices cannot be told to be the same people; about 22 cents an hour, twice that with the long requests; the noises it marks are left out); without OpenRouter it takes YouTube's own captions, without speakers. A file goes the same way, its sound written down as it is. `sentences.txt` numbers the sentences (they end at . ! ? and where the voice changes).
2. **`episode.mjs moments`** has Gemini 3.8 Flash read `sentences.txt` once (about ten cents an hour), so that the agent's chat does not carry the whole video through every later step. Its rubric is [Melvynx' `/clips`](https://github.com/Melvynx/video-agent-skills) (MIT):
   - what the video holds: claims, stories, numbers, mistakes, how-tos, answers. Under four in all it is too thin for clips;
   - each moment a range of whole sentences, at most 59 s (it fits every post type Storrito offers), scored 0–2 on five points: a **cold** open that works as a hook in its first three seconds, **one** idea, a clean **end** after the payoff, leaning on nothing outside it (**alone**), and its **length** (2 from 20 s, 1 from 15 s; the tool counts this one). Under 7 of 10 a moment is listed as `weak`;
   - a hook for the screen (6 words or fewer, never a label) where the opening is weak;
   - the ideas too spread out over the video to cut, worth recording anew.

   `moments.json`: `{ inventory, moments: [{ first, last, kind, title, why, score, points, weak, hook, crosstalk, start, end, seconds, opening, link }], ideas }`, best first.
3. **`episode.mjs cut`** downloads only the stretch (up to 4K: a 9:16 window of a wide shot is a third of its width) with a breath of air around it, or cuts it from the file.
4. **`frame.mjs`** cuts footage wider than the frame to it, on whoever speaks. The shots are the footage's own, to the frame: `lib/cuts.mjs` compares every frame at its own time over one frame up to 2.2 seconds, so a hard cut (also a very dark one), a talk show's push wipe of a quarter second, a dissolve and a fade through black are one cut each, measured against the picture's own motion around it (ffmpeg's scene score found 4 of 10 cuts on Hot Ones' dark set). A change of only part of the picture (someone standing up), one whose picture comes back within a second (a hand before the lens) and a small one count only where the model names a cut; a flash inside one shot is none, a flash into another picture one; no shot is shorter than six frames. Gemini 3.8 Flash then watches the clip once (a small copy without the file's tags) with stills of every shot (its first, middle and last frame, one every three seconds between; three to seven cents a clip) and gives each shot's kind and, on every still, every face with the voice it speaks with, its box (ear to ear), the box of its eyes, nose and mouth, and where it looks (left, right, the camera; asked for both eyes, the far one of a three-quarter view too: on twenty stills of the guest's close-ups the boxes were right on all and the direction on all; a face called "camera" with its features to one side of its box leans a little that way). The window sits on the face of the voice heard most, a third from the top, a turn under 1.5 s (a "ja", a laugh) moves nothing, a reaction shot shows who it shows; between the stills the face is followed in the frames (`lib/track.mjs`: ten a second, forward from one still and back from the next, no model; a still the frames from both sides do not lead to is the model's slip, left out and listed; two faces crossing go straight from still to still; a face missing on a still is out of the picture, not cut), and the window holds it all through: still where it can, wider where that is enough, else following it lazily (it rests while the face keeps 3 % inside, goes along when it pushes, and never turns back within a second unless the face itself would leave). Of a close-up (a face wider than half the window) the window holds the eyes, nose and mouth, never closer to the edge than 6 % of the window's width (best-takes' punch-in takes 5.4 % of each side), and keeps three quarters of the head's width in the picture where the features allow; room on the side the face looks to (18 % of the face's width more than behind, the window's middle on that side of the features') takes what the head leaves, at most half of the window's play; the back of the head, an ear, hair at the side may be cut, as the channel's editors and OpusClip cut them. Between two stills the features keep their place in the head's box, which the tracker follows; where the head turns between them they go from the one place to the other. The room is taken as it is on average over two seconds, so a swaying head moves nothing; only the features about to come closer to the edge than those 6 % move the window at once. The numbers were chosen on the five passages of our three-way test (with more room a swaying close-up left the window too little play, 0.65 turns a second; with less, the far eye of a three-quarter view and a pair of glasses came to the edge in the rendered clips; without the head's share half the frame stood empty in front of a face at a sway's end, with 80 % the window followed the sway again). An answer without the features (a `clip.people.json` from before) falls back to the middle 80 % of the face's box (`FRAME_CORE=0.9` before `clips.mjs reframe` or `frame.mjs` changes that share), which in a three-quarter view lets the nose or an eye go. A face low in a wide shot (an empty wall above it) gets a closer window, down to 55 % of the footage's height; a wide shot of the room in which someone of the talk speaks is cut to them even when Gemini calls it B-roll or split, as long as Gemini says the picture shows the people, not material (whole, their face would be 2 to 5 % of the frame's height; the channels' editors and OpusClip cut to the speaker there). Slides, a screen or a product with the speaker small beside them stay whole; where Gemini does not say, the shot stays whole and the list says how to cut it to the speaker. The tool checks its own windows on every still and at each piece's first, middle and last frame and names, with a ⚠, every face whose eyes, nose or mouth are cut by an edge or closer to it than 1.5 % of the width (a small face: the whole face, with 2 %); only an ear, a cheek or hair at the edge of a close-up is listed without a ⚠, as accepted; a face half out of the footage itself is not the window's. Each close-up says how calm its window is (turns a second) and how much of its time the features are in with their room (`closeup` of the piece in `framed.json`). What a window would cut apart is shown whole, above the captions over a blurred, darker copy of itself: B-roll (a photo, archive footage, a place, a document, a screen; Gemini names each shot's kind and the box that must stay visible) and a picture split into feeds side by side. Two who speak, too far apart for one window, go one above the other (two panels from the top, the captions on the lower one's chest): a split screen of two voices always, one shot of both while they talk back and forth (three turns or more of under five seconds). Without OpenRouter the footage stays centred. `framed.mp4` keeps the footage's timing (a picture that starts a frame after its sound holds its first frame) and a keyframe every second for the preview's seeks. The shots (each person's track, the stills with `parts`: the features as shares of the face's box and `lean`), the windows (a close-up's with `closeup`: `turns`, `held`, `inside`) and the faces at an edge (`part`: `features`, a fault, or `side`, accepted) are kept in `framed.json`; `framed.sheet.jpg` (eight shots a sheet, drawn cell by cell so that a Sprite's memory holds it; a cell without a frame is grey and named) shows per shot the footage with the window drawn in and the frame at its first, middle and last frame, red where eyes, nose or mouth are at an edge; `clips.mjs reframe <clip> --whole <s>` / `--follow <s>` (one window on who speaks, never two above each other) changes a shot's kind without asking the model again and keeps the edit.
5. **`clips.mjs prepare`** runs the three above for one moment and hands the framed clip to [best-takes](../best-takes/) (`takes.mjs new --aspect 9:16 --pauses tight --punch on`): ElevenLabs Scribe writes the clip's words again, word-exact, "äh"s included; every pause over 0.4 s goes whatever the picture shows; at every jump cut the picture goes 12 % closer or back out, closer on a key word, never on a piece shown whole or two above each other (`takes/source/still.json`); one keep of the whole moment goes into `takes/cut.json`, from the first word heard whole to the last (the cut's air may hold the tail of the sentence before). From there the edit is best-takes': `build` (with `--hook` for the card), `captions`, `check`.
6. **`clips.mjs place`** (prepare runs it once; again after the cut is built, as it builds again) puts the text where the faces are not, by agentitos' [layout](../layout/) (`place.mjs text`) from every face of every shot as the framing tracked it, in the frame (`lib/faces.mjs`: through each window, shown whole, as two panels):
   - **one place for the captions of the whole clip** (published Shorts keep theirs in one place within a video, their middle at 60–75 % of the height, one line of about three words, the type 4 % of the height): every height of the safe box at the usual type and at 90 and 80 % of it, the one with the least on a face, then the least raised, then the usual place and size;
   - **each shot raised** that needs it, so that the chin and the margin below it clear the captions (`frame.mjs --lift`: the window lower in the footage where it reaches further, the rest over a blurred band at the bottom, under the apps' bars), once for the whole shot, by its lowest moment, and only while the whole head stays in the picture (`--raise moderate`, the default: the box's top and layout's margin for the hair below the frame's top); `--raise full` goes on until the eyes reach the safe box's top, the head cut by the frame;
   - **never on the mouth**: where nothing may be raised the captions lie on the chin or neck, below the face's mouth line (65 % of its box), the type smaller first; said per shot as not avoidable without cutting the head;
   - **the hook** (layout's two kinds) where it clears every face over its seconds and the captions: a short text below the chin or above the head, else a card at the top; a card in the text's place where the picture behind it is not a close-up (hands, things, a photo); it ends at a cut when the shot after leaves it no place;
   - what still lies on a face, per shot, with what would avoid it; a place of its own for a shot only proposed (two seconds on a face saved at least), never taken.

   It writes `takes/place.json` (the captions' options for agentitos' captions, the hook's kind and box), `takes/source/faces.json` (the faces in the frame, for best-takes' punch-ins, which grow away from the captions, and for its check, which names text on a face), `placement/text.json` (layout's whole answer) and `placed.sheet.jpg` (per shot its first, middle and last frame with the captions yellow, the hook cyan, the faces red). `--caption-y`, `--font-size`, `--lift <s>:<px>` and `--hook text|card` fix one choice and are kept; what `reframe` and `place` asked of the framing is kept in `reframe.json`, so that one does not undo the other. [`references/text.md`](references/text.md) says what it decides and what is left.

   Measured on five passages of two channels (2026-11-18, the framing with eyes, nose and mouth; the mouth line from them), the framing alone → moderate → full: captions on the mouth or above 3.8 → 0.1 → 0 s (robe), 0.6 → 0.4 → 0 (ice-bowl), 12 → 1.4 → 0 (deaths); on chin or neck only 10.2 → 7.6 → 0.6, 8.6 → 4.8 → 0, 20.6 → 25 → 1.5 s; the hook on a face 1.2 and 2.9 s before, 0 in both; the face's box over the frame's top in deaths 15.9 → 18.2 → 36.3 s, the band at the bottom 0 → 0 → up to 363 px. Moderately the type fell to 54 px in robe and deaths (the usual 77 px reached the mouth more often); the two podcast clips had their captions off the faces already.
7. **`clips.mjs render`** (`render-all <clips.json> <folder>` for all, one after the other on a Sprite, two at once from 16 GB; about twice the clips' length (1.5 to 2.4 times measured from 4K footage, more on a busy machine); `prepare-all <episode> <clips.json>` prepares them side by side, as many as the memory holds) renders the built preview with HyperFrames (6 Mbit/s) and runs best-takes' `finish`: −14 LUFS, a true-peak limit, the subtitles as `.srt` beside the video.

`kinetic-lyrics` uses the same `episode.mjs` and `frame.mjs` (its `tools/` run them from here) and puts every word big on screen instead.

## Limits

- A clip is only as good as its moment: a video full of "as we saw", "this slide" gives few clips and more ideas to record anew.
- The framing follows faces and voices and shows B-roll, photos and split screens whole, smaller, above the captions; a picture in a picture is cut as one picture.
- A subject that moves without speaking (a car, a player, an animal) is followed by [auto-reframe](../auto-reframe/) (CPU tracking, offline), not by this framing, which goes by faces and voices.
- Only material the person may use.
