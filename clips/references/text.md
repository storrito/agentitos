# clips: where the text goes

`clips.mjs place work/<k>` hands every face of every shot, ten times a second, to agentitos' layout (`place.mjs text`) and takes what it gives back. Read this before you change what it chose.

## What the tool decides, and why

- **One place for the captions in the whole clip.** Captions that move at every cut make the eye hunt for them; in 240 published Shorts the captions keep one place within a video, their middle at 60–75 % of the frame's height, below the chin in 88 % of the frames with a face, one line of about three words in 77 %, the type 4 % of the frame's height (77 px in 1920). That is what is usual, not what is shown to work. The tool tries every height of the safe box at that type and at 90 % and 80 % of it, and takes the one that leaves the least on a face.
- **A shot whose chin would reach into the captions is raised, moderately.** Its window goes lower in the footage where the footage reaches further down, else the picture goes up over a band of its own blurred, darker copy at the bottom, which lies under the apps' bars (the username, the caption, the sound), below the safe box: the list says how tall it is per shot. It goes up only while the **whole head stays in the picture**: the top of the face's box and the margin layout keeps above a head for the hair stay below the frame's top. A head cut by the frame is a fault in itself; the channels' editors and the commercial services leave a tight close-up as it is and let small text lie on the chin or neck. A shot is raised once for its whole length, by its lowest moment: a picture that rose and sank with every nod would read as a shaky camera. A shot shown whole or as two panels is never raised.
- **Never on the mouth.** Where no raise is allowed, the captions stay at the clip's place and may lie on the chin or the neck, never above the face's mouth line (65 % of the face's box from its top: on eleven stills of talk-show and podcast close-ups the model's box put the mouth at 65–85 % of its height). Where the usual type would reach it, the type goes smaller first (90, 80, 70 % of the usual), for the whole clip.
- **`--raise full`** raises until the eyes reach the safe box's top: the chin is clear, but the frame cuts the head's top (in a tight close-up the hair goes, the eyes stand at an eighth to a quarter of the height) and the band at the bottom grows to 300–400 px. Only where the person prefers a clear chin to a whole head.
- **The hook** goes where it clears every face over all of its seconds, beside the captions: a short text in two lines below the chin (where published Shorts put it, in the middle of the frame), else above the head, else a card at the top. White type reads only over a calm picture: the framing knows faces, not hands or things, so only a close-up (every shot under the hook with a face at least 28 % of the frame tall: below its chin a shirt) counts as calm; over a wider shot, a picture shown whole or two panels the hook is a white card in the same place. The list says which kind and why. When the first shot changes within its time to one that leaves it no place, it ends at that cut (from 1.2 s on).
- **best-takes' punch-ins** (12 % closer at a jump cut) grow from below where they used to, so that a chin that clears the captions still clears them closer in and a mouth line above them stays above them; moderately raised, never so that a head inside the frame goes over its top, and not at all lower where the frame cuts the head already.

## What it proposes, and what is left

- A `✗` line per shot where the captions still lie on a face: how long, how deep into the face (up from the chin), why (how much higher it would have to stand than it may) and what would avoid it.
- A `note:` where a shot would keep its captions off a face with a place of its own, saving more than two seconds on the face. It is not taken: a change of place costs the reading the one place gives. Tell the person when it is a long shot and they care.
- **Tight close-ups** (a face a good half of the frame tall and more, the chin at 70–90 % of the height, as a talk show's guest close-ups are) have no room below the chin inside what the apps leave free, and their head reaches the frame's top already. Moderately they are not raised: the list says "on the chin or neck, not avoidable without cutting the head or a smaller picture". That is no conflict to fix. Measured on three talk-show clips: 6–27 s a clip on chin or neck, 0 s on a mouth.

## When to change it

- The person wants the chin clear and accepts a head cut by the frame: `--raise full`. The person says one shot's face sits too high: `--lift <second>:<px>` with fewer px for that shot (0: as framed).
- The person wants the captions higher (the usual middle of the frame) or smaller: `--caption-y <px>` (where the lowest line ends) or `--font-size <px>`; the tool raises the shots around it. Never below the safe box's bottom: there the apps lay their own text.
- The person wants the hook as a card (a reply, a part of a series): `--hook card`.
- The boxes of the faces come from a model on stills, followed in the frames: on a profile or a turned head they can be off by a tenth of the face. A `✗` or a raise that looks wrong on the sheet: look at the frame, and change the lift by hand.
