# clips: where the text goes

`clips.mjs place work/<k>` hands every face of every shot, ten times a second, to agentitos' layout (`place.mjs text`) and takes what it gives back. Read this before you change what it chose.

## What the tool decides, and why

- **One place for the captions in the whole clip.** Captions that move at every cut make the eye hunt for them; in 240 published Shorts the captions keep one place within a video, their middle at 60–75 % of the frame's height, below the chin in 88 % of the frames with a face, one line of about three words in 77 %, the type 4 % of the frame's height (77 px in 1920). That is what is usual, not what is shown to work. The tool tries every height of the safe box at that type and at 90 and 80 % of it (69 and 62 px; 70 % would be 54 px, under the floor below), and takes the one that leaves the least on a face. One line for a style of a few words, two for a style of whole phrases (clean, subtitle, fill, label); `--lines 1|2` changes it.
- **The area is the style's ink.** Once the captions are built, the area it reserves is the built style's line, its box, and for a style without a box the outline and shadow below the line (Hormozi at 62 px: 65 px of line and 8 px of shadow; Chrome measured its ink 61 px tall); the lowest line ends that much above the area's bottom and the lines are narrower by the outline at each side, so the ink stays inside the area and the safe box. Before the captions are built it is as tall as the tallest style's: place again after `takes captions`.
- **Never on the mouth.** A face's lines come from its eyes, nose and mouth as the framing knows them (the model's three boxes, followed with the face; where they do not hold, the head's proportions): the nose line 70 % down them, the lip line 88 % down (the middle of the mouth), the mouth line their bottom and 15 % of their height more (an open mouth's lower lip drops below the box of a still, and between two stills a head that tips down carries its mouth lower in its box: on the rendered frames of three talk-show clips the lower lip stood up to 7, 8 and 28 % of that height below the features' bottom in nine frames of ten, up to 43 % in one close-up that tipped down). Text below the mouth line lies on the chin or neck; between it and the lip line on the lower lip; above the lip line it covers the mouth, above the nose line the nose or the eyes. Where the usual type would reach the lower lip, the type goes smaller first, for the whole clip, never below the low end of published captions: 61 px, a line 3.2 % of the frame's height (layout-data's study of 188 Shorts: the median 4.0 %, the middle half 3.2–4.9 %). On the lower lip at that size the list says "not avoidable at a readable size", nothing to fix; it names a smaller type only where one would clear the lip at that place, with its size. Between two stills the eyes, nose and mouth are an estimate that rides with the head's box: a mouth that opens wide or a head that tips down there is not seen. Where a lower lip comes from such an estimate (more than 0.25 s from a still), the list says so with the two stills ("between the stills at 23.4 and 25.4 s"): look at those seconds in the preview; captions on an open mouth there are a `✗` the tool could not see (in robe, 2026-11-18, the list's lower lip at 22.75 s was an open mouth).
- **A shot whose chin would reach into the captions is raised (`--raise mouth`, the default).** Its window goes lower in the footage where the footage reaches further down, else the picture goes up over a band of its own blurred, darker copy at the bottom, which lies under the apps' bars (the username, the caption, the sound), below the safe box: the list says how tall it is per shot. It goes up only while the **whole head stays in the picture** (the top of the face's box and the margin layout keeps above a head for the hair stay below the frame's top), except where the captions would otherwise cover a mouth: then it goes up past the head's top until the mouth is clear, but never with the eyes above 27 % of the frame's height (the upper end of where published Shorts have them: the eye line's middle half 27–38 %, layout-data's study; above it the frame starts at the brows); where that is not enough the captions lie on the lower lip, said (the list says "past the head's top so that the captions keep off the mouth"). A shot is raised once for its whole length, by its lowest moment, and every return of the same close-up stands at the same height: a picture that rose and sank with every nod or every return would read as a shaky camera. A shot shown whole or as two panels is never raised.
- **`--raise head`** never cuts a head: a shot goes up only while its whole head stays in the picture, the type goes to its floor first, and the captions lie on the chin or the lower lip where that leaves no room (said, with `--raise mouth` as what would avoid it); a punch-in that would lift a head the frame cuts goes less close or stays as it is. **`--raise full`** raises until the chin clears, as far as the eyes reach the safe box's top: the frame cuts the head's top (in a tight close-up the hair goes, the eyes stand at an eighth to a quarter of the height) and the band at the bottom grows to 300–400 px. Only where the person prefers a clear chin to a whole head.
- **The hook** goes where it clears every face over all of its seconds, beside the captions: a short text in two lines below the chin (where published Shorts put it, in the middle of the frame), else above the head, else a card at the top. White type reads only over a calm picture: the framing knows faces, not hands or things, so only a close-up (every shot under the hook with a face at least 28 % of the frame tall: below its chin a shirt) counts as calm; over a wider shot, a picture shown whole or two panels the hook is a white card in the same place, also where no place clears the faces. The list says which kind and why. When the first shot changes within its time to one that leaves it no place, it ends at that cut (from 1.2 s on).
- **best-takes' punch-ins** (12 % closer at a jump cut) never undo the placement: a face's nose, lip and mouth lines that stood above the captions stay above them, ones below sink no further; where only the eyes going under the apps' top bar would allow that, the piece goes 6 % closer or stays as it is (build says which).

## What the list says, and what is left

- Per shot of the cut: how far it was raised and why, and where the captions lie, the part named with its seconds: on no face; on the chin or neck; on the lower lip and the chin ("not on the mouth: not avoidable at a readable size", with the seconds of it that are an estimate between two stills); above the head, on the hair; above the eyes, on the hair or forehead (layout takes that only where every other place lies longer on eyes, a nose or a mouth, or where `--caption-y` put them: give a lower one); `✗ on the mouth` or `✗ on the nose or eyes` with a sentence that says what would avoid it. A `✗` is something to fix: a cut to another shot there, a smaller type by hand (`--font-size`), a larger lift than one you fixed, or (a picture shown whole) `reframe --follow`. The check (`takes check`) names the same parts with the same seconds.
- A `note:` where a shot would keep its captions off a face with a place of its own, saving more than two seconds on the face. It is not taken: a change of place costs the reading the one place gives. Tell the person when it is a long shot and they care.
- `✗ Captions below the low end of published ones`: a group the captions set smaller than 61 px because one word is wider than the line, named with its size. Put a soft hyphen (`­`) where the word may break into its text in `cut.json` `"fixes"` (`"Verwaltungs­wissenschaftler:innen"`), then `place` again with `--lines 2`: it breaks there on two lines at the clip's type.
- **Tight close-ups** (a face a good half of the frame tall and more, the chin at 70–90 % of the height, as a talk show's guest close-ups are) have no room below the chin inside what the apps leave free, and their head reaches the frame's top already. Moderately they keep their whole picture with the captions on the chin or the lower lip, and go up past the head's top only for the mouth. Measured on three talk-show clips and two podcast clips: see the README for the figures.

## When to change it

- The person wants every head whole (a look, a hat, the hair) or minds the band at the bottom: `--raise head`. The person wants the chin clear and accepts a head cut by the frame: `--raise full`. The list says per shot how far the frame cuts a head at the top (px and seconds, and how much of it the raise adds): the price of each. The person says one shot's face sits too high: `--lift <second>:<px>` with fewer px for that shot (0: as framed).
- The person wants the captions higher (the usual middle of the frame) or smaller: `--caption-y <px>` (where the area ends; the safe box's bottom at most, the tool refuses lower: there the apps lay their own text) or `--font-size <px>`; the tool raises the shots around it.
- Two lines (a style of phrases set in one, a long word): `--lines 2`; one: `--lines 1`.
- The person wants the hook as a card (a reply, a part of a series): `--hook card`.
- The boxes of the faces come from a model on stills, followed in the frames: on a profile or a turned head they can be off by a tenth of the face; the framing lists with ⚠ the stills whose eyes, nose and mouth did not hold. A `✗` or a raise that looks wrong on the sheet: look at the frame, and change the lift by hand.
