# What building the skills taught

The evidence behind the rules, and the lessons that are not rules yet. [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) and [standards.md](standards.md) stay short because the reasons are here.

## Keeping this page

- An entry is something that went wrong, or that a person or a reviewer found, and that would go wrong again in another skill. What concerns one skill alone goes into that skill's README. Never general craft.
- An entry has what holds, the evidence (the date, the pull request or the run, what was measured or seen) and where it lives now: a rule, a tool, a test, or "open".
- The agent that builds or changes a skill adds its entries when it is done, and after reading a bot's run of the skill, each under the heading it belongs to. Two entries that say the same become one, with both pieces of evidence.
- An open entry that a second skill runs into becomes a rule in AGENTS.md or standards.md, or better a check in a tool or a test, in a pull request that says so. A rule nobody can find the reason for, or whose reason is gone, is taken out together with its entry.
- An entry whose lesson lives in a tool's check or in a test alone is removed: the pull request keeps its evidence. Entries stay as the reasons of rules written in text, and while they are open.

## Tools

- **A long step goes on where it stopped.** 2026-11-02, a bot's screencast: `new` on three sources of six minutes ran into the shell's limit of 180 s and began again from nothing. Lives in: screencast-cut's `new` (#46), standards.md section 4.
- **Time is measured on the file as it plays, not as it decodes.** 2026-11-02, the same run: Chrome's Opus in WebM has gaps in its time stamps, a file of 380.1 s decoded to 376.3 s, the words and levels were on the decoded clock and the cuts landed inside words. 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): its new sound for a video came out shorter than the picture by the gaps (6.0 s under 7.5 s of picture on a made-up file), found only when the standards asked for files as devices write them. Lives in: screencast-cut resamples with `aresample=async=1:first_pts=0` (#44), voice-sound reads every source that way (#111), standards.md section 1. Open for any other tool that reads a browser's recording.
- **What the agent builds itself is the tool's gap.** 2026-11-02, the same run: the bot joined two recordings, made tighter crops and invented word numbers for footage without speech, with packages it installed. Lives in: screencast-cut (#46), standards.md section 3.
- **The same case can take eight minutes or sixty.** 2026-11-16 and 18, the lab: story-intros' case of two Stories passed with 49 tool calls in 8.4 minutes and with 114 in 59.5. Why was not looked into. Lives in: standards.md section 5 (the waits are read from every run, and the tool calls with them). Open: the cause.

- **One job had been done three times before it had a skill.** 2026-11-19, voice-sound (#111): best-takes and screencast-cut each levelled or cleaned a voice in their own code; its README's table sets the three side by side on the same recordings. Lives in: standards.md section 2.
- **A step with a model in it is noisy: one run says little.** 2026-11-18, clips (#65): one run of the moment selection, scored against editors' picks, was too noisy to compare; means of three runs were compared, and a holdout of three episodes was spent after one use. Open.
- **A Sprite shows little free memory and has much.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): a Sprite of 8 GB with nothing running showed 0.9 GB available, because it is given its memory as it uses it (`balloon_inflate` less `balloon_deflate` in /proc/vmstat: 6.6 GB held back). A tool that sized its work by the available figure ran one piece at a time; with the balloon counted, five minutes of sound took 51 s instead of 101 s. Lives in: voice-sound's `memoryMb`. Open for any other tool that sizes its work by free memory.
- **A loop that corrects toward a measured value needs bounds.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): its level stage lowered its ceiling whenever the encoded file's true peak was over it; ffmpeg's AAC encoder with its default coder lifted one spot by 2.5 dB whatever the ceiling, and the loop crushed a five-minute recording to chase it. Three reviews had passed it; the fourth, on ordinary clips only, found it. Lives in: voice-sound's level stage (three bounds, and `-aac_coder fast`, whose true peak follows the ceiling). Open for any other skill that writes AAC under a true-peak ceiling.
- **A tool says what it did not have.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111) as a plugin on the machine of another chat host than rragent: the machine had no neural denoiser, the tool used ffmpeg's method and said nothing of it, and both agents ran `which` to find out. Lives in: voice-sound's noise step; standards.md section 6.

- **A long text for the agent arrives with a hole in it.** 2026-11-20, two skills as they are on a machine whose agent reads a skill as the output of a call and is shown 10,000 characters of an output by default: with captions (12,959 characters) the agent read a part of the text again by hand, and with voice-sound (#111) each of two runs lost a step to the cut. 14 of the 25 skills were longer that day, layout the longest with 32,985. The same job took the agent 20 steps with captions' text as it was and 13 to 16 with one of under 2,000 characters written for that machine. Lives in: AGENTS.md, "What goes into a SKILL.md".

## The flow the person sees

- **A button that only leads to a question is a question asked twice.** 2026-10-28, a bot's run of publish-video: after a click on "Another style" it asked which style; it also ticked all 22 accounts and asked whether to fix two frames of overlapping subtitles in a finished video. Lives in: AGENTS.md, "In the chat" (the buttons); publish-video's SKILL.md (the accounts, the small flaws).
- **A hook shows a result that works.** 2026-11-02: a screencast opened on what was missing in the product, and the person rejected it. Lives in: screencast-cut's hook rule (#46). Open for the other skills that write a hook.

## What to build

- **Looks and features are read off what is published, not off what tools advertise.** 2026-11-17, captions (#63): 198 published Shorts classed by the look of their captions; 147 carried a look the four styles could not make, and looks the caption tools advertise (neon glow, typewriter) hardly appeared. Eight styles were added from the count. Open.
- **Every part earns its place.** 2026-11-18, captions (#63): the twelve styles were compared on seven traits; none was a copy of another, three older ones were improved instead of kept as they were for their own sake. Lives in: standards.md section 2.

- **A video for the owner is built with the skills.** 2026-11-19: two explainer films were made with hand-written generators (739 lines for one) and repeated a fault, music pumping in every breath, that the skills' level rule avoids. Built with the skills, a video is also their test, and its gaps are fixed in them. Lives in: standards.md section 2.

## Testing

- **The machine you built on is not the one it runs on.** 2026-11-17: a test Sprite had no emoji font and a caption card's emoji rendered as an empty box. 2026-11-18, the lab: the laptop's ffmpeg 4.4 lacked an option screencast-cut needs and gave other loudness numbers than a Sprite's ffmpeg 8. #116: the tests had run on ffmpeg 9, which a bot's machine does not get. Lives in: testing.md section 3; the tests' ffmpeg is the bots' (#116).
- **An independent review finds what the author's own checks pass.** 2026-11-18, captions (#63): a second review found three defects before a commit, `/code-review` fifteen before the merge, among them a crash on a number as a word's text. Lives in: standards.md section 9.
- **A picture in a skill is not a person's recording.** 2026-11-17, captions: the picture of the styles first showed a crop of a test recording; it was made again on a drawn backdrop before it went into the repository. Lives in: testing.md section 2.
- **Reviews find rarer and rarer cases.** 2026-11-19, the layout work: every pull request took three to five hours over two or three review rounds that kept finding the same classes of fault. 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): three independent reviews each found rarer cases than the one before, and the worst finding of the third was a fault of the second round's fix. Lives in: standards.md, "The bar" and section 9.
- **An average hides what a table per kind shows.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): one average over 205 recordings read as hardly better; per kind of recording it showed that on noise that changes ffmpeg's filters did almost nothing and the denoiser a lot. Lives in: standards.md section 10.
- **Heavy work on the machine everyone shares slows everyone.** 2026-11-18: a render of 5 s took 451 s on a laptop that other sessions were loading, and 33 s on a Sprite. Lives in: testing.md section 3.
- **A skill's text is read on the machine it will run on.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): two runs with a stand-in agent on the machine of another chat host than rragent (the machine's own system prompt, its REPL the only way to act) took five evaluations each where three would do: its SKILL.md says "You run in rragent", writes into `artifacts/` and describes buttons that machine does not have, and it is longer than the 10,000 characters the machine shows its agent, so its middle was cut. The lab's cases, which play an rragent bot, passed and showed none of it. Open: the skills' texts for a host other than rragent.
- **Two columns with the same numbers are a fault to find, not a result.** 2026-11-20, voice-sound (#111): a marker changed in an engine's command lost its space, the variable that named the denoiser was never set, and a whole comparison ran without the thing compared; it showed only as two identical columns. Open.
