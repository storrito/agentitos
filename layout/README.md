# layout

How a recording goes into a frame (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9), and where captions and a hook can go: computed from where the faces are, or the part of a screen that matters, and from what the apps lay over the video. It tries every fit (the picture as it is, zoomed, smaller over a ground, cropped, cropped as a band, as a panel at one side, two people stacked, a screen between the text's bands with a webcam's circle, a screen above a webcam's panel), checks each against the zones, ranks them and says why. It builds no video and decides nothing: an agent gets the candidates and a picture of each to look at, and chooses.

> Agents: start with [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md).

```bash
node tools/place.mjs grid take.mp4 placement/demo/grid.jpg --at 3      # a frame with a labelled 10 % grid, to read the boxes off
node tools/place.mjs place placement/demo --frame 9:16 --source 1080x1920 --face 340,420,400,540 --text caption --text hook
node tools/place.mjs page placement/demo --still take.mp4 --at 3       # the first three candidates as pages and pictures, checked by platform-zones (a face that moves: at each moment)
node tools/place.mjs captions placement/demo --candidate 1             # the options that set agentitos' captions into its caption area
node tools/place.mjs text placement/clip --frame 9:16 --shots shots.json --text caption --text hook   # a clip placed already: one caption place over every face of every shot
node tools/place.mjs text placement/clip --frame 9:16 --shots shots.json --text area --area-top 1025   # large type instead: one area below every mouth
node tools/place.mjs beside placement/card --frame 9:16 --source 1280x720 --face 555,150,180,195 --material-min 600x340 --text caption   # a card beside a speaker who stays in the picture
node tools/test.mjs                                                    # 152 tests; --quick: the 138 that need no Chrome and no ffmpeg
node --test tools/test-cases.mjs                                       # the 50 stored cases (cases/): what text, place, beside and captions write and print, unchanged
node tools/cases.mjs write [case…]                                     # a change that is meant: store the new outputs, the difference goes into the pull request
node tools/cases.mjs against origin/main                               # what this checkout's code gives otherwise than main's, case by case (--cases <folder>: cases of your own)
node tools/sweep.mjs sweep/                                            # for people who change the weights: which fit wins where
```

Requirements: Node 20+, no npm packages. agentitos' [platform-zones](../platform-zones/) beside this skill (`PLATFORM_ZONES` naming its `tools/zones.mjs`, else beside it, else in the machine's clone): every zone comes from it, and its `layout` command is the checker. ffmpeg with ffprobe for `grid` and for a frame of the recording in `page`. Chrome for `page`, found as platform-zones finds it (`CHROME`, else on the PATH, else one puppeteer downloaded). No model, no key.

## Where it sits

No tool of this collection sees a face. Each skill that frames people (clips, best-takes, screencast-cut) has its own rule for where the picture goes, and the captions always end on the safe box's bottom, which in a close selfie is the speaker's chin. This skill is the step between knowing what a recording is and building a video from it:

1. footage-kind names the situation (a talking head, two people in one picture, a desktop with a webcam). It is on a branch of its own, draft pull request 58, and not needed to run this: an agent's look at a frame tells as much.
2. An agent reads the boxes of the faces, or of a screen's region, off `grid`'s picture.
3. `place` computes the candidates, `page` shows them and has platform-zones check them, the agent chooses, by [`references/situations.md`](references/situations.md) where two fit.
4. The numbers go to the skill that builds the video, and `captions` prints the options for [captions](../captions/).

**captions needs its placement options for that** (`--x`, `--y`, `--max-width`, `--align`, `--font-size`, `--lines`). They are on captions' branch `captions-place` (draft pull request 61), not on main yet. A captions without them ignores them and sets the captions on the safe box's bottom; it answers "Placed where asked: …" only when it took them.

The caption area is as tall as the block captions builds into it: its lines, a style's line height apart, and what a style's box adds above and below. For the type and line count handed over it is the tallest block of captions' twelve styles (eight of them on captions' branch `captions-styles`, not on main; the figures were read from its commit 7c5b5bf of 2026-11-18 and checked again there: its changes to `karaoke`, `hormozi` and `wipe` in f6c0639 move the pill's padding sideways and change how words group and how wide they count, not a block's height, so the area below holds; a later change of a style's line height or of what its box adds above and below changes `caption.line` and `caption.padEm` here): `clean` for one line (1.3 em and 0.44 em of padding), `clean` and `label` alike for two. The figures are copied, as `caption.line` and `caption.padEm` in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`; a style that pads more needs `--set caption.padEm=…`. Against captions' `render.mjs` (the top of the area it reports: its lines a style's line height apart and the padding of its box, on both branches), at 77 px type: one line of `clean` fills the area to the px (134 px), one of `label` leaves 9 px of it free; two lines of either fill it (234 px). A card of its own (captions' `box`, only where one is asked for) is taller: its padding and a border of 3 px or more at the style's line height overrun the area by 6 px in `clean` and by 15 px (one line) or 25 px (two) in `label`; where cards are wanted, `--set caption.padEm=0.8` holds them in every style. An area narrower than a long word needs at its type (`caption.longWord`, 9 em) carries a note on its candidate: captions sets such a word smaller for its moment, and how its grouping breaks around it is captions' own.

## What goes in

Boxes are `x,y,w,h` in px of the source: the top left corner, then width and height. A face runs from the hairline to the chin and from cheek to cheek; without a hairline (bald, a cap) from where it would be, as far above the brows as the nose's bottom is below them; in profile from the front of the ear to the tip of the nose. Text keeps a margin around the box: above it 35 % of its height, for the skull or the hair. Every `--face`, `--face-at`, `--face-track` and `--region` belongs to the `--source` before it.

## What comes out

`place` prints the candidates and writes `<folder>/placement.json` (`--json` prints the same):

```json
{"frame": "9:16", "size": [1080, 1920], "platforms": ["Instagram Reels", "Instagram Story", "TikTok", "YouTube Shorts", "Facebook Reels"], "fallback": true,
 "source": [1080, 1920], "faces": [[340, 420, 400, 540]], "needs": [{"role": "caption", "lines": 1}, {"role": "hook"}], "notes": [],
 "candidates": [{"fit": "full", "scale": 1, "left": 0, "top": 0, "zoom": 1, "enlargement": 1, "window": [0, 0, 1080, 1920],
                 "faceInFrame": [[340, 420, 400, 540]],
                 "text": [{"role": "caption", "where": "below-chin", "on": "picture", "box": [192, 1306, 696, 134], "lines": 1, "font": 77, "style": "cap", "align": "center"},
                          {"role": "hook", "where": "below-chin", "on": "picture", "box": [192, 1003.2, 696, 238.1], "lines": 2, "font": 96, "style": "text", "align": "center"}],
                 "problems": [], "hard": 0, "soft": 0, "unmet": [], "ground": 0, "faceHeight": 540,
                 "terms": {"enlargement": 0, "ground": 0, "subject": 0, "soft": 0, "text": 0, "hook": 0}, "cost": 0, "score": 100,
                 "why": ["no hard problem", "every text need met: 1 caption line below-chin, hook text at 96 px below-chin", "the source's pixels are not enlarged (1×)",
                         "the face is 540 px tall (28 % of the frame's height)", "the picture fills the frame", "cost 0 (no term)"]}],
 "defaults": {}, "asked": {"frame": "9:16", "platforms": []}}
```

- `scale` is frame px per source px; `left`, `top` where the source's top left corner lies in the frame; `window` the part of the frame the sharp picture fills (the rest is ground). `stacked` and `side-by-side` carry `parts`, one such placement per half; a screen with a webcam a `circle` (its `box` in px of the frame, and `crop`, the square it shows, in px of the picture `of` names: `webcam`, its own file, or `screen`, the screen recording a webcam is baked into).
- `problems` are hard (`✗`: under what an app covers, out of the frame, text on a face or on text, a face cut by its picture's edge, the source enlarged over the forbidden limit, the circle on the region), soft (`!`: in the safe box's margin, cut by a feed's crop, enlarged over the soft limit, another person cut by a crop's edge) or unmet (`–`: a text without a free place, a screen's text too small).
- `tracks` (a face that moves): its usual box, its hull, how much larger the hull is, which moment gives the hull each edge, and `decides`: the moments without which the first candidate would be another or placed otherwise, each with `says`, what changes in words.
- `onGround` (a candidate of its own, named `…, text on the ground`): where the picture stands smaller than an upright frame, the same picture with its hook and captions on the ground above and below it. `groundShows`: what the ground is (the pages' blurred, darker copy of the picture, 1.17× larger than covering the frame) and, for each text on it, whether a face of the picture lies there as a blur.
- `alike`: candidates that showed the same frame to the eye and are listed once, as this one (their fit, scale and how many px they lay from it).
- `notes` say what no candidate can; a candidate's own `notes` say that its caption area is narrower than a long word.

`page` writes `candidate-N.html` and `candidate-N.png` for the first three candidates (or `--candidate 2`, `1,4`, `all`) and, with `--still`, `still.jpg`: one frame of the recording, without any of the file's tags. A face that moves gets a picture of every candidate at every moment: `--still <video>` takes the moments its boxes were read at, or the `--at` given (several allowed); pictures of one source go one `--at` each, in their order. The files carry the moment (`candidate-1-at-2.5.png`, `still-at-2.5.jpg`). Two sources (two people, a screen and its webcam) take a `--still` each, in their order (`still-2.jpg`): the webcam's circle and panel show its picture, cut as the candidate says; without its still the circle is drawn. The picture is taken in a window taller than the frame and cut back to it, so that it is the frame with any Chrome (one that counts its own bars into the window left the lowest rows black). What `page` wrote is listed in `placement.json` (`written`): the next `page` or `place` into the folder removes exactly those files, never another one, and never a file that is passed in (a still that already lies in the folder is used as it is; a file of the caller's under the name of a frame page would write is refused, not written over). `grid` writes the one picture it is given a name for, and refuses to write over the file it reads. Nothing is written into the skill.

## Material beside the speaker

`place.mjs beside` answers "the speaker is here, material of free shape must show too: where does each go?" for another skill (b-roll's frame "beside": a number, a quote, a screenshot for two to four seconds while the speaker stays visible). It is a command of its own, not an option of `place`: its input is no second recording with boxes but what the material needs, and its answer is no placement of a source but a field for each of the two and the change between them. Its candidates carry the keys `page` and `captions` read, so both work on its folder. The code is `tools/lib/beside.mjs` (the geometry) and `tools/lib/beside-page.mjs` (its pages).

| option | what |
| --- | --- |
| `--frame`, `--source`, `--face` (or `--face-at`, `--face-track`: one face) | the speaker's footage and face, as for `place`; `--face assumed`: the usual framing of a talking head (a fifth of the frame tall, in the middle, a third down, as the footage covers the frame), marked as assumed in every candidate |
| `--platform reels,tiktok` | the apps the video is built for: their zones, as every skill takes them (none: the frame's usual ones) |
| `--caption-area area.json` | the captions' band from captions' `build` (its `area.json`: `left`, `width`, `top`, `bottom`, `size`, `lines` in px of the frame, the line boxes without a shadow below them), or its six values `192,696,1238,1440,96,2` |
| `--caption-style hormozi` | the band of a captions style, measured by running captions' `build` on placeholder words (lines from `--text caption:N`, else the style's); neither: the layout skill's own band (the tallest of captions' styles at 77 px). `captions.band` says which |
| `--material-min WxH` | the smallest field the material reads in, px of the frame |
| `--material-ratio 16:9` | the shape it prefers: the field is then the largest box of that shape in the room |
| `--material WxH` | a picture or clip of fixed size: the largest box of its shape, its `scale` given, over 2× a hard problem |
| `--text caption`, `caption:2` | captions only: a hook stands before the material shows (`place`) |
| `--before scale,left,top[,x,y,w,h]` | the speaker's placement just before, as `place` hands it over, with its window where it did not fill the frame; without it the source covering the frame on the face is assumed |
| `--set beside.face=0.22`, `beside.faceUpTo=2` | the face's target in the panel and the column (0: the usual 18 %, in both; 0.12 to 0.5), and how far the footage may be enlarged for it (0: the soft limit, `weights.enlarge.softFrom`; never past `forbiddenFrom`, 2×) |
| `--set beside.wish=0.22`, `beside.wishUpTo=1.25` | a larger face taken only while the footage stays under its limit (0: the soft limit); where it needs more, as much of it as stays under, and the target at the least. None by default |
| `--set beside.side=left`, `beside.captions=stay` | 16:9: the column's side fixed (default `look`), the captions left where they stand without the material (default `move`: under it) |
| `--set beside.faceMin=0.1` | its own defaults (`faceMin` 0.12, `column` 1/3, `look` 0.1, `pipCrop` 2, `face` 0, `faceUpTo` 0, `wish` 0, `wishUpTo` 0, `side` look, `captions` move, `coverUpTo` 0 (34 %), in `lib/beside.mjs` with their sources) and any of the solver's |

**What a caller gets by default:** the face towards 18 % in a panel and in the 16:9 column (the column aimed at 23 %, the lower end of published talking heads filling the frame, until b-roll's first builds: with the wish set and no `beside.face` it enlarged a medium shot to 1.53× in place of keeping it sharp at the base; one base lets the caller's three figures hold everywhere), covering the panel's width up to a face of 34 %, never enlarged past 1.5× for it (where that is less, the face stays smaller and `speaker.target.heldBy` says why), the layout skill's caption band, the frame's usual apps. **b-roll's values**, settled by its owner on built videos: `--set beside.faceUpTo=2 --set beside.wish=0.22 --set weights.enlarge.softFrom=1.25 --caption-area <its captions>/area.json --platform reels` (in 16:9 also `--set beside.side=left --set beside.captions=stay`): 22 % while the footage stays under 1.25×, else as much of it as does and 18 % at the least, up to 2×, soft past 1.25×, in the column as in the panel.

A feed's or grid's crop (the Instagram feed's 4:5, the profile grid's 3:4) is a note in `beside` (`crops`) and a soft problem in `place`: `place` chooses where its text and picture go and its ranking keeps them inside those crops where it can, so a crop there is something to act on; `beside`'s fields already stay inside them wherever they keep their size there, and what remains is nothing a caller can change.

It always answers: where the target cannot be had it takes the most the panel holds and the footage gives, where no arrangement gives the material its field it says so in a note and returns the best candidates with their problems, and there is always a panel (`shape: "rect"`) among them. It exits only on input it cannot read.

What it reuses: the face below a screen at 18 % of the frame's height (`screenFace.height`) as the panel's target, the margins around a face (`free`), the captions' block and type (`caption`), the circle's sizes (`circle`), `holdAxis` for the hold of the footage, the weights for enlargement, ground and a face short of its target, and `judge` for every candidate, so that `page` has platform-zones check its pages as any other. It keeps no zone of its own.

The arrangements, ranked hard problem, unmet need, then the frame's usual order, then cost:

- **9:16, 4:5, 1:1: `beside-above`, then `beside-pip`.** Above: the speaker as a panel across the frame's foot, the face towards 18 % of the frame's height without enlarging past 1.5×, covering the panel's width where that fits, also with the face over its aim while it stays at most 34 % of the frame's height (`beside.coverUpTo`, 0: `weights.subject.to`: the third quartile of the faces of published talking heads and podcast clips, a face published videos keep; the spread of faces below a screen, 17.1–19.4 %, is that of the aim itself and would take the full-width panel from ordinary takes at 20 %), its face then where it was across (else smaller, in the panel's middle, ground on both sides: also a face that covering would make larger than 34 %, brought to its aim, unless that ground were a sliver under 24 px a side; b-roll's close-up in 4:5 and 1:1 without captions stood at 40 %, now 22 %); the captions on the seam, and `speaker.card` the window below them, so that they stand on the panel's ground; the captions on the seam; the material gets all the frame above the seam, inside the safe box or the wide stretch above the buttons and inside every feed's crop where it keeps its size. The panel takes as much height as the face needs at its target and never leaves it under `faceMin` (12 %: the median face of frames with a small picture of the speaker in a screen); where the material needs more height the panel is not built or stands behind the circle, and a note gives the largest field it holds. Pip: the material over the frame between the safe box's top and the captions at its bottom, the speaker as a circle (screencast-cut's size: 22 % of the width in 9:16, 16 % of the shorter side in 4:5 and 1:1) bottom left (9:16) or top right, showing a square two faces tall around the face (inside the footage where face and head then lie in the disc, else on the face with ground where the footage ends), judged as a disc with the margin above the head.
- **16:9: `beside-column`, then `beside-above` where the material keeps its field, then `beside-pip`.** The column: a third of the width over the frame's full height, on the side from which the speaker looks at the material (a face more than a tenth of the source's width off its middle is taken to look towards the other side; in the middle: left), the footage covering it at the scale it had full-frame where that covers, else covering it with the face at the panel's target (18 %, the wish while the footage stays under its limit, up to `beside.faceUpTo`); the material on the other two thirds, the captions under it. Nothing in 16:9 was measured.

`screen-stacked`, given the card as a 1920×1080 screen, had put it 828 px wide (x 60–888) and failed it as too small to read, and offered only a circle in the other frames. With `--material 1920x1080` beside gives it 960×540 in 9:16. A picture of fixed size is never shown past 1.5× its pixels, and one shown under two thirds of the area another arrangement gives it stands behind that one.

`speaker.scale`, `left` and `top` place the footage itself (frame px per footage px, its corner in frame px); `speaker.host` is the same placement as a transform of the footage filling the frame (object-fit: cover), as b-roll moves its host. `place` and the other commands still take the layout skill's own caption band, not `area.json`: decided against for this round, as they search caption places of their own.

Every candidate's `change` holds `before` and `after` (`scale`, `left`, `top`, `window`, `face`; `round` for the circle), `face` (`dx`, `dy`, `zoom`) and `says`; SKILL.md says what a calling skill does at the change. From the first video built with it (b-roll's draft pull request 80; 9:16 and 16:9 looked at, 4:5 and 1:1 only computed): captions moved with the picture crossed the speaker's eyes for about 0.2 s, so that skill fades them out and in at their new place in the middle of the change, moves the picture in a straight line over 0.5 s, and keeps the ground around the speaker opaque from the start, its opening moving with the video. Experience from one video, not a measured rule.

## Two checks

The search judges thousands of placements, too many to open a page for each: `tools/lib/judge.mjs` applies the rules of platform-zones' layout check to the solver's own boxes, with the letters' place estimated from the font's metrics. `page` then has platform-zones itself measure the candidate's page in Chrome, by its letters, and prints both verdicts side by side. The checker takes 2 px off every side of a box before it judges, and so does the solver: a box up to 2 px past an edge counts as on it for both. They agree on every page of the tests (about 120) and on the sweep's winners (below). On 320 random inputs (every frame, a size as `720x1280`, sources of nine shapes, one or two faces, a moving face, two files, a screen, named platforms) all 1,017 candidates as pages got the same verdict from both; one finding differed, on a candidate that fails anyway (a face 0.65 px past the frame's edge after the slack, which the page rounds to a whole px). The letters' place is an estimate within about 3 px: on a 720×1280 frame the checker found a hook text 2 px into a feed's crop where the solver found it clear, a soft finding, the verdict the same. What only the solver judges, because the checker cannot know it: how far the source is enlarged, a face cut by its own picture's edge, a text need without a place, a screen's text too small, another person showing in a crop or a half, the circle on the region.

## The weights

Three things rank before any cost. A candidate with a hard problem stands behind every one without. One that leaves a need unmet stands behind every one that meets them all. And where no speaker is named, a crop that leaves a person out of the picture stands behind every candidate that shows them all: whom to leave out is the caller's decision (`--speaker`), no weight makes it. Among the rest the smaller cost wins, in points, from [`tools/weights.json`](tools/weights.json) (every term with a sentence of what it says); `--weights file.json` takes another file, `--set weights.enlarge.softFrom=1.4` changes one term.

| term | value | what it does | where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `enlarge.softFrom` | 1.5× | up to here enlarging the source costs nothing | judged: 1.5× looked crisp |
| `enlarge.forbiddenFrom` | 2× | over this a candidate fails | a guess between 1.875× (judged fine) and 2.67× (judged too soft) |
| `enlarge.costAtForbidden` | 3 points | the cost at the limit, rising in a straight line from `softFrom` | set so that the judged order comes out; from about 3.3 the crisp square would pass the mildly soft 4:5 crop, and nobody judged which of the two should lead |
| `subject.from`, `subject.to` | 23 %, 34 % | a face this tall, in per cent of the frame's height, costs nothing | measured: the middle half of the faces in 49 talking heads and 60 podcast clips of 240 published Shorts (medians 26 % and 29 %) |
| `subject.smaller` | 0.3 a % | for every per cent a face falls short of the band | set so that the judged order comes out |
| `subject.larger` | 0.05 a % | for every per cent a face is over the band; no reward for a larger face | a guess, kept small: a close take zoomed in a little stands before the same take made smaller over a ground |
| `subject.regionEnough` | 50 % | a screen's region is the better the more of the frame it spans, up to this | a guess, no case decides it |
| `ground.cost` | 0.1 a % | for every per cent of the frame the sharp picture does not fill | set so that the judged order comes out |
| `soft.cost` | 0.5 | for a soft problem other than enlargement | a guess |
| `text.away`, `text.hookSmaller` | 0.05, 0.02 | for a text away from its usual place, a hook under its size | small on purpose: they only order holds of the same picture |

**They rest on one judged case, one measurement and guesses.** The case: a 1280×720 take of one person into 9:16 with two caption lines, stills looked at by people: the 4:5 crop at 1.875× first, the square at 1.5× second, the whole picture as a strip third (the person tiny), the crop that fills the frame at 2.67× too soft to publish. The two crops are a quarter of a point apart. The measurement: how tall a face is in published Shorts. It replaced a reward for a larger face up to half the frame's height, which nothing published supports. It says what creators do, not what works: in that study nothing separates the videos that did well from the others (5 of 69 comparisons under p = 0.05, 3.5 expected by chance). There is no term for how much of the picture a crop cuts away. Candidates less than a point apart are not told apart by anything measured.

**Some figures rest on the first use on real footage** (2026-11-18: an agent placed twelve recordings by the documents alone, and what it found wrong was fixed). They are defaults in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`, each with its source, and rest on these twelve cases only:

- `insetFace` (19 %): an inset goes below half the picture's size (`insetMin`) while its smallest face stays this tall; the lower end of the middle 80 % of faces in published talking heads, not one of these cases. A take with a face half its height, a hook and two caption lines passes only so.
- `free.above` (0.35): what is kept clear of text above a face's box, a share of its height, for the top of the head. On the stills the head's top lay 0.22, 0.23, 0.31 and 0.335 of the box's height above it (bald and frontal, short hair, grey hair in profile, bald in profile: there a hook card had ended on the skull); hair worn high reached 0.49 and is not held. The upright take laid out by hand has a box read from the top of the head, so its cases keep the margin it has (`hand.json`). With this margin two people stack less often: a face over about a third of a half's height no longer fits a half with the captions on the seam.
- `same` (2 % of the frame's shorter side, 2 % of scale): two candidates whose pictures, windows, circles and text lie this close are one; the later is named in the first's `alike` and the next different one takes its place. Pairs 9 and 18 px apart looked the same in their pictures, pairs 193 and 218 px apart did not. `--all` lists them all.
- `together` (2), `togetherSize` (0.67): two people of one picture stand close together when their faces are about the same size (the smaller at least two thirds of the larger: at the same distance from the camera), their boxes at most two mean face widths apart across and at most one face's height up or down. Stacked would split them into two halves of the same shot; it stands behind every candidate that shows both in one picture (walking side by side: 1.0–2.3 widths; four in a row with room between them: 2.2–3.8; two at a table: 12.9).

The type sizes come from the same study (a caption line 4 % of the frame's height, one line; a hook text 12.4 % of the height tall in the middle of the frame): they are defaults in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`, each with its source. [`references/situations.md`](references/situations.md) has the figures with their counts.

### The sweep

`node tools/sweep.mjs <folder> [--weights mine.json] [--no-check] [--jobs N]` (`--jobs 1`: one checker and one Chrome at a time, for a machine busy otherwise) is for whoever changes the weights or the defaults. It runs the solver on 16,128 made-up faces (15–55 % of the source's height, the eyes at 25–60 % of it, up to 15 % of the width off the middle; an upright and a wide source into each of the four frames; one caption line, two, a hook, a hook and two lines), has platform-zones check every winner's boxes and 384 winners as pages, and writes `<folder>/sweep.json` (9 MB) and `<folder>/sweep.html`: one map per case and text need of which placement wins for which face, the thresholds in words, and, with `--weights`, how many winners the other weights change against the skill's own and from what to what. Two to five minutes on a machine with 20 cores, ten seconds without the checker; `--html` only rebuilds the page.

It says what the weights do, not what looks good. Moderate changes move little: with enlargement at 5 points at the limit instead of 3, no winner of the grid changed.

## Text over a clip placed already (`text`)

A clip of several shots whose picture another skill placed (clips' framing: a window on whoever speaks, shots shown whole, two panels; best-takes on its own, which places each video with `place` first and gives each as one shot) needs no candidates for the picture, only where its text goes, given every face over time. `text` takes the shots (`--shots shots.json`: each `start`, `end`, `weight` (its seconds in the cut), `faces` as tracks of `{ t, box, feat? }` in px of the frame (`feat`: the box of eyes, nose and mouth), `room` how far its picture may be raised without a ground, `null` for none at all) and the hook's time, and gives back (`<folder>/text.json`, `lib/placed.mjs`):

- **One place for the captions of the whole clip.** A caption that jumps from shot to shot reads badly, and published Shorts keep theirs fixed within a video (the layout study: the middle at 60–75 % of the height, below the chin in 88 % of the frames with a face, one line of about three words in 77 %). Every height of the safe box in steps of half a per cent, at the usual type (`caption.font`, 77 px in 1920) and at 90, 80 and 70 % of it as long as that is not under `caption.font.least` (61 px: the low end of published captions, a line 3.2 % of the height in the study's 188 Shorts; so 69 and 62 px, not 54), one line (or the lines asked for), centred and as wide as `place` makes them. The area is `caption.line` em a line and `caption.padEm` em more: by default the tallest style's (1.3 and 0.44); a caller that knows the style sets both (clips: the style's line height, and for a style without a box the ink of its outline and shadow below the line), so that the area is what the ink takes.
- **A clip without a face** (a screen, a product, b-roll): nothing keeps the captions from the usual place, so they stand on the safe box's bottom at the usual type, their middle inside 60–75 %; `caption.where` is `no-face` (else `below-chin` or `above-head`), and the hook stands in the middle of the frame (layout's hook text, a card where the caller says the picture is not calm). Whether they lie on the material (a screen's text) the tool cannot know: it knows faces only.
- **Each shot's lift**: how far its picture goes up so that every face's keep-out (the box and the margins of `free`, the same as `place` keeps) clears the captions at every moment; once for the shot, by its lowest moment. At most what its `room` allows without a ground plus a ground that stays under the apps' bottom (below the safe box), never so far that a face's eyes (`eyes` of its box) reach above the safe box's top, and, by the caller's `--raise`: `head`, not so far that the top of a face's box and the margin above it for the hair (`free.above`) go over the frame's top (a head cut by the frame is a fault of its own); `mouth` (the default; `moderate` is its old name), as head, never with the eyes above `eyeLine` (27 % of the height: the upper end of the eye line's middle half, 27–38 %, in layout-data's 240 Shorts), and past the head's top where the captions would otherwise lie on a mouth or a lower lip and that costs less than the seconds it saves (`past`); `full`, until the chin is clear. Each shot says how far the frame cuts a head at the top (`headCut`: px at most, seconds, and how much of it the lift adds). The returns of one framing (the same face at the same size and place: the camera cut back to a close-up it showed before) stand at one height, that of the one that needs most: a close-up that came back at 144, 363, 225, 97 and 150 px read as a camera that moves.
- **The lines of a face**, from the caller's `feat` (the eyes, nose and mouth, a box per moment beside `box`), else from the box by the head's proportions (`IMPLIED`: the eyes' top at 38 %, the mouth's bottom at 86 % of the box, the low end measured on stills whose features were right, so that a line is never above the real one): the nose line 70 % down `feat`, the lip line 88 % down (the middle of the mouth), the mouth line its bottom and 15 % of its height more (`OPEN`: an open mouth's lower lip drops below the box a model gives of a still, and a head that tips between two stills carries its mouth lower in its box; on the rendered frames of three talk-show clips the lower lip stood up to 7, 8 and 28 % of that height below the box in nine frames of ten). Captions reaching above the mouth line lie on the lower lip (1,000 points a second: a smaller type or another height comes first; what remains at the floor is said as not avoidable at a readable size, a `note:`, with the largest type that would clear it at that place and lift, computed for the shot (`clearsAt`, px; 0 where the lower lip reaches below the captions' bottom and no type would), and whether `--raise mouth` would where `head` was asked), above the lip line on the mouth, both lips (20,000 a second: a fault, a conflict, after a raise past the head's top as above), from the nose line up to the eyes' top with the brows on the eyes or the nose (100,000 a second, a conflict); above the eyes' top, inside the head's box, on the hair or the forehead (50,000 a second, a `note:`, not a conflict: it covers no eyes, nose or mouth; half the eyes' points and two and a half times a mouth's, so that captions, which belong below the chin, never leave a chin or a lower lip for a forehead, nor a mouth for as long a forehead; until 2026-11-19 everything from the head's top down to the nose line counted as the eyes); below the mouth line on the chin or neck (a `note:` where no lift may clear it). Above a head, within the margin kept for the hair: said as on the hair. The eyes' top is the top of `feat` (clips' framing asks the model for the eyes with their brows); without `feat` it is `BROWS`, 15 % of the box down: the high end of where the model's eyes with their brows began on 220 stills of the three-way talk-show clips (0.29 of the head's height in the median, above 0.15 on one still in twenty), so that the eyes are never taken to begin lower than they do (the proportions' 38 % lay below the brows' top on three stills of four). Captions on such a head's hair or forehead in that band from 6 % down are said as where its eyes may begin, its eyes not known (`maybe`), weighed as the forehead. Each part counts only where the text overlaps it, as wide as the head's box (`PARTS`, `partOf` in `lib/placed.mjs`).
- **The cost** that ranks the places (`COSTS` in `lib/placed.mjs`, guesses set so that a second of text on a face outweighs the rest): 10 points a second on a face's keep-out, 30 times how deep into the face from its chin the area reaches (a share of the face's height) a second, the parts' points above; raising a shot 1 point a second for every tenth of the frame's height by its window, 4 over a ground; a middle outside 60–75 % 1 point for every per cent and every ten seconds of the clip; smaller type 1 a px and ten seconds. The smallest wins; the next four are listed.
- **A place that changes at a cut** is only proposed (`changes`, a `note:`): for a shot of four seconds or more where another height saves more than two seconds on a face. Taking it is the caller's.
- **The hook** over its seconds on every shot it spans, raised as they are, beside the captions: `layoutText`'s own kinds and order (a text below the chin, above the head, a card at the top), its keep-outs the faces of every moment in its time; where the caller says the picture behind it is not calm (`"hook": {"calm": false}`: it knows the faces, not hands or things), a text's place takes a card, also where no place is free. Where no place is free and the first shot changes within its time, it ends at that cut (from 1.2 s on); else the least bad of its usual places (of the kind asked for, where one was): never on the captions where another is not, then on no eyes, nose or mouth where another is not, whatever the share of the face it covers (best-takes' tight take: a text in the middle covered 12 % of the face, on the mouth, and was taken before; a card at the top covers 16 %, on the forehead and the hair: for a hook the hair or the forehead is mild, a title over the hair is what editors do where a head fills the frame), then the least on a face; on a head known by its box alone, whose eyes may begin anywhere from `BROWS_HIGH` (6 % of it: the highest the model's eyes box began on those stills, a head tipped back) down to where the proportions put them, a place that reaches into that band is said with its seconds (`onFace.maybe`) and weighed after the places on the features, before the rest (counted as the eyes, a card on the hair tied with a text on the mouth below it, and the text won by its smaller share); said with why (`kindWhy`) and with what it lies on (`onFace.features`). Where the captions' best place leaves the hook worse off than another of their places that lies on the faces no longer (above a head that fills the frame there is room for captions or a hook, not both: best-takes' phone take had its captions above the head and the card over them), the captions take that place and a `note:` says so; never where the caller fixed `--caption-y`.
- **What still lies on a face**: per shot who, from when to when, the seconds on each part (`on`: nose (the eyes or the nose), hair (the hair or the forehead), mouth, lip, chin) and how deep; of the lower lip's seconds those whose features the caller marks as an estimate between two stills (`between` on a track's moment; `lipEst` and the `stills`), said in the note, never weighed: a mouth that opens between two stills is not seen; a conflict (✗) only where something can still be done (a cut to another shot, a smaller type by hand, a larger lift than one fixed, a framing that follows the speaker), a note for what the rules leave. `--caption-y` below the safe box's bottom (where the apps lay their own text) or above its top is refused.

- **Large type in one area** (`--text area`, `lib/area.mjs`; kinetic-lyrics' type, phrase after phrase across the safe box's width): no caption line but one area from the safe box's bottom (`--area-bottom`) up, never higher than the caller's usual top (`--area-top`: where it sets its type without faces) and never smaller than `area.least` (15 % of the height: kinetic-lyrics' engine sets four lines at its smallest words in about 14 %, and a lockup grows by 4 % while it shows). Its top is one for the clip, tried from the usual top down in steps of half a per cent. "Clear of a face" is what large moving type needs: never on the eyes, the nose or the mouth with the lower lip (the mouth line, as for the captions); on the chin, the neck and the body below it may lie (`area.chin`, 1 point a second); on the hair or the forehead of a face whose eyes stand below the area as little as captions (50,000 points a second, said, not a fault). The area goes lower or a shot is raised, whichever costs less (`area.smaller`: 3.6 points for every per cent of the height and ten seconds, 0.9 of a raise over a ground for the same per cent and seconds, so that the area goes lower first where the shot that needs it holds nine tenths of the clip, and a shorter shot goes up first; with the 1 point of smaller captions a reporter's close-up of 14 s in 22 s took a quarter smaller type rather than a raise of 38 px), a shot raised by the mouth line of its lowest moment (by `--raise` as for the captions, and past the head's top unless `head`: the area's only reason to raise is the mouth), the returns of one close-up at one height; on the mouth, the lower lip or the nose stays a `✗` with its seconds and what would avoid it. `--top` fixes the top. A sample of a track stands half the gap to its neighbours, for captions and large type alike; it is cut to 0.26 s (or the track's usual gap, where larger) only where it stands alone in a track dense elsewhere in the shot, so a shot with one sample or a track evenly sparse counts as it always did. Tested on made-up tracks (a face high above it, a mouth in the usual area, one below the smallest area's top with `full` and `mouth`, two shots that disagree, a shot shown whole, returns, no face, the command and its refusals) and on three kinetic shorts (kinetic-lyrics' README).

Tested on made-up tracks (`test.mjs`: a face high, low, a tight close-up no lift clears, a close-up raised past its head's top for its mouth, one whose eyes allow no raise (captions over the whole mouth: a conflict, never "the lower lip"), a clip without a face, the lower lip's note naming a smaller type only where one clears it, captions and a hook card on the hair or the forehead (the review's head from y 900 to 1420 with its eyes from 1098 and captions at 976–1080: on the hair or forehead, a px into the brows on the eyes; captions that keep to a chin or a mouth rather than a free forehead), large type over a forehead, a lower lip from features that are an estimate between two stills said as one, returns of one close-up at one height, two shots that disagree, a shot that cannot be raised, a face that moves under the captions, faces low everywhere, the hook below the chin, the hook's fallback over a busy picture, the hook ending at a cut, the command and its refusals) and on five passages of two channels through clips (2026-11-18, after an independent review; caption ink from Chrome against the parts a model found on the rendered frames): moderately raised, on the nose or eyes 0 s in all five, on the mouth 1.5 s in one talk-show clip (0.5 s in another before, now 0), the type 77 px in all five (62 px in two before) where tight close-ups go up past the head's top, ink outside the safe box 0 s (up to 17.5 s before). The cost's figures are guesses; none was judged against another by people.

## What it cannot do

- **See.** The boxes are an agent's reading of a grid; a wrong box is a wrong layout, with every check passing. Whether the picture behind a text is calm, it never knows.
- **Measure words.** A caption block and a hook are taken as wide as their area; the pages show placeholder words (caption lines of three words, a hook of two short lines). The video's own words are checked where the video is built.
- **Follow a face.** A moving face gets one placement for the whole clip, by its hull; following it, or cutting the clip where it moves, is only suggested in a note. Moments that give the hull no edge are not tried as deciding.
- **Time.** `place` and `beside` lay out one moment of a video; `text` places a clip's text over time where the picture is placed already, but never chooses the picture of a shot. Which layout a part gets, how long a hook stays and what changes when it leaves are the agent's.
- **Name tags, step texts**: not placed. A second picture beside a person is `beside`'s; `stacked` is two people.
- **`beside`: what nobody measured.** 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9 (the figures are from upright Shorts with a screen, not cards over a talking head), the column's third, which way a speaker looks, the circle's crop, the 12 % below which a panel gives way, and how long a move between full-frame and panel should take. An upright take with a large face fills no panel's width in 4:5, 1:1 or 16:9: the footage stands smaller with ground beside it, which passes and looks weak. No column in 4:5 or 1:1. A close-up in the 16:9 column fills the column's height at its own scale and keeps a face larger than its aim (40 % from a face 40 % of the source): bringing it down would leave ground above and below it, and nobody looked at that.
- **Other subjects than faces and screens.** A product, a hand, a thing shown: [auto-reframe](../auto-reframe/) follows one moving subject.

## The code

`tools/place.mjs` is the command line. `tools/lib/`: `placed.mjs` (text over a clip placed already, over time), `area.mjs` (large type in one area over such a clip), `beside.mjs`, `beside-page.mjs` and `caption-area.mjs` (material beside the speaker, its pages, the captions' band from captions), `solve.mjs` (the cases, the ranking and its reasons), `fits.mjs` (the fits of one picture), `parts.mjs` (two people in two halves), `screen.mjs` (a screen and a webcam's circle), `evaluate.mjs` (one placement judged and costed), `text.mjs` (where the text finds room), `judge.mjs` (the zones' rules on the solver's boxes, and the comparison with the checker), `page.mjs` (a candidate as a page), `png.mjs` (a picture cut back to the frame), `zones.mjs` (platform-zones as a program), `defaults.mjs` (what is assumed, and the weights), `boxes.mjs` (the geometry), `sweep-page.mjs` (the sweep's page).
