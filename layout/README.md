# layout

How a recording goes into a frame (9:16, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9), and where captions and a hook can go: computed from where the faces are, or the part of a screen that matters, and from what the apps lay over the video. It tries every fit (the picture as it is, zoomed, smaller over a ground, cropped, cropped as a band, as a panel at one side, two people stacked, a screen between the text's bands with a webcam's circle, a screen above a webcam's panel), checks each against the zones, ranks them and says why. It builds no video and decides nothing: an agent gets the candidates and a picture of each to look at, and chooses.

> Agents: start with [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md).

```bash
node tools/place.mjs grid take.mp4 placement/demo/grid.jpg --at 3      # a frame with a labelled 10 % grid, to read the boxes off
node tools/place.mjs place placement/demo --frame 9:16 --source 1080x1920 --face 340,420,400,540 --text caption --text hook
node tools/place.mjs page placement/demo --still take.mp4 --at 3       # the first three candidates as pages and pictures, checked by platform-zones (a face that moves: at each moment)
node tools/place.mjs captions placement/demo --candidate 1             # the options that set agentitos' captions into its caption area
node tools/place.mjs text placement/clip --frame 9:16 --shots shots.json --text caption --text hook   # a clip placed already: one caption place over every face of every shot
node tools/test.mjs                                                    # 75 tests; --quick: the 65 that need no Chrome and no ffmpeg
node tools/sweep.mjs sweep/                                            # for people who change the weights: which fit wins where
```

Requirements: Node 20+, no npm packages. agentitos' [platform-zones](../platform-zones/) beside this skill (`PLATFORM_ZONES` naming its `tools/zones.mjs`, else beside it, else in the machine's clone): every zone comes from it, and its `layout` command is the checker. ffmpeg with ffprobe for `grid` and for a frame of the recording in `page`. Chrome for `page`, found as platform-zones finds it (`CHROME`, else on the PATH, else one puppeteer downloaded). No model, no key.

## Where it sits

No tool of this collection sees a face. Each skill that frames people (clips, best-takes, screencast-cut) has its own rule for where the picture goes, and the captions always end on the safe box's bottom, which in a close selfie is the speaker's chin. This skill is the step between knowing what a recording is and building a video from it:

1. footage-kind names the situation (a talking head, two people in one picture, a desktop with a webcam). It is on a branch of its own, draft pull request 58, and not needed to run this: an agent's look at a frame tells as much.
2. An agent reads the boxes of the faces, or of a screen's region, off `grid`'s picture.
3. `place` computes the candidates, `page` shows them and has platform-zones check them, the agent chooses, by [`references/situations.md`](references/situations.md) where two fit.
4. The numbers go to the skill that builds the video, and `captions` prints the options for [captions](../captions/).

**captions needs its placement options for that** (`--x`, `--y`, `--max-width`, `--align`, `--font-size`, `--lines`). They are on captions' branch `captions-place` (draft pull request 61), not on main yet. A captions without them ignores them and sets the captions on the safe box's bottom; it answers "Placed where asked: …" only when it took them.

The caption area is as tall as the block captions builds into it: its lines, a style's line height apart, and what a style's box adds above and below. For the type and line count handed over it is the tallest block of captions' twelve styles (eight of them on captions' branch `captions-styles`, not on main; the figures were read from its commit 7c5b5bf of 2026-11-18 and checked again there: its changes to `karaoke`, `hormozi` and `wipe` in f6c0639 move the pill's padding sideways and change how words group and how wide they count, not a block's height, so the area below holds; a later change of a style's line height or of what its box adds above and below changes `caption.line` and `caption.padEm` here): `clean` for one line (1.3 em and 0.44 em of padding), `clean` and `label` alike for two. The figures are copied, as `caption.line` and `caption.padEm` in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`; a style that pads more needs `--set caption.padEm=…`. Against captions' `render.mjs` (the top of the area it reports: its lines a style's line height apart and the padding of its box, on both branches), at 77 px type: one line of `clean` fills the area to the px (134 px), one of `label` leaves 9 px of it free; two lines of either fill it (234 px). A card of its own (captions' `box`, only where one is asked for) is taller: its padding and a border of 3 px or more at the style's line height overrun the area by 6 px in `clean` and by 15 px (one line) or 25 px (two) in `label`; where cards are wanted, `--set caption.padEm=0.8` holds them in every style. An area narrower than a long word needs at its type (`caption.longWord`, 9 em) carries a note on its candidate: captions sets such a word smaller for its moment, and how its grouping breaks around it is captions' own.

## What goes in

Boxes are `x,y,w,h` in px of the source: the top left corner, then width and height. A face runs from the hairline to the chin and from cheek to cheek; without a hairline (bald, a cap) from where it would be, as far above the brows as the nose's bottom is below them; in profile from the front of the ear to the tip of the nose. Text keeps a margin around the box: above it 35 % of its height, for the skull or the hair. Every `--face`, `--face-at`, `--face-track` and `--region` belongs to the `--source` before it.

| what | options | what the solver does with it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| one face | `--source 1080x1920 --face 300,570,600,900` | every fit that holds the face in the safe box and leaves the text room; from a source with pixels to spare (one that covers the frame below 1×, as 4K into 1080, of the frame's shape or not) also a crop or zoom closer in (`closer`, on the safe box's middle with the margins beside the heads), the smallest face brought towards 23 % of the frame's height (`subject.from`) without enlarging the source past 1.5× (`enlarge.softFrom`) |
| several faces in one picture | `--face … --face … [--speaker N]` | the fits that hold them all; in a frame narrower than the picture also a crop on one person (on each, or only on the speaker) and, for two, `stacked` |
| two people as two files | `--source A --face … --source B --face …` | `stacked` in an upright frame, `side-by-side` in a wide one, both in 4:5 and 1:1; the smaller face is brought towards the larger where that does not enlarge its file into what is soft |
| a face that moves | `--face-at 0:300,570,600,900 --face-at 2.5:…` (every `--face-at` of one source is the same face), or `--face-track file.json` (`[{ "t": 0, "box": [x, y, w, h] }, …]`; two people who both move: one such list each, `[[…], […]]`) | placed for the hull of its boxes, so that the placement and the text hold for the whole clip; its size in the ranking is its usual (median) box; the moment that decides the placement is named |
| a screen | `--region 700,300,480,300 --min-text-height 14 [--readable 24]` | the region in the safe box at the smallest scale at which its smallest text reads (a capital 24 px tall in a frame 1080 px wide, a guess), the screen running to the frame's edges between the text's bands |
| a screen and a webcam | a second `--source WxH --face …` (a webcam as a file of its own), or a `--face` with the screen's own source (a webcam baked into the screen recording: the circle shows that part of its picture, and where the screen's picture shows it too the speaker shows twice, a soft finding) | the webcam as a circle in a free corner of the safe box, sized as screencast-cut sizes it, smaller or in another corner where the region is; and in an upright frame `screen-stacked`: the screen in the upper half at the size that reads, the webcam as a panel under it (at most half the frame, the face towards 18 % of its height without enlarging past 1.5×, smaller where the face would not stand clear of the captions, down to a picture half the frame's width with ground beside it; a face short of 18 % costs as a face short of its band; a screen whose region would be wider than the safe box stands narrower than the frame), the captions on the seam, a hook as a card above; a baked webcam too small for a panel half the frame wide gets none, and a note |
| captions | `--text caption` (one line), `--text caption:2` | an area for N lines: 77 px type in upright frames (a line 4 % of a 9:16 frame's height, as published Shorts set theirs: usual, not shown to work), 50 px in 16:9 (a guess); below the chin on the safe box's bottom, else in the picture's bottom, beside the face (16:9), above the head |
| a hook | `--text hook`, `--text hook:text`, `--text hook:card` | in an upright frame first a short text of two lines below the chin, above the captions (96 px, down to 72; its middle at 53 % of the frame's height where the face leaves that free: what published Shorts do, which is usual, not shown to work), then the same above the head, then a card of two lines across the wide stretch (76 px, down to 64: platform-zones' pattern); `hook:text` and `hook:card` name one kind; a screen and two people in two parts keep the card; in 16:9 two big lines beside the face (112 px, down to 72) |
| the frame | `--frame 9:16` (or `4:5`, `1:1`, `16:9`, a size as `720x1280`), `--platform reels,tiktok` | the zones of those platforms; none named: the frame's usual ones together. Every size in px is for a frame 1080 px on its shorter side and is brought to the frame's |

`--set key=value` changes one default for one run (`hookCard.minFont=76`, `caption.padEm=0.6`, `facePad=0`; they are in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`; a key that is not there, a value of another kind, or a search step under 0.001 is refused), `--all` lists every fit also where the unchanged picture passes.

## What comes out

`place` prints the candidates and writes `<folder>/placement.json` (`--json` prints the same):

```json
{"frame": "9:16", "size": [1080, 1920], "platforms": ["Instagram Reels", "Instagram Story", "TikTok", "YouTube Shorts", "Facebook Reels"], "fallback": true,
 "source": [1080, 1920], "faces": [[340, 420, 400, 540]], "needs": [{"role": "caption", "lines": 1}, {"role": "hook"}], "notes": [],
 "candidates": [{"fit": "full", "scale": 1, "left": 0, "top": 0, "zoom": 1, "enlargement": 1, "window": [0, 0, 1080, 1920],
                 "faceInFrame": [[340, 420, 400, 540]],
                 "text": [{"role": "caption", "where": "below-chin", "on": "picture", "box": [192, 1306, 696, 134], "lines": 1, "font": 77, "style": "cap", "align": "center"},
                          {"role": "hook", "where": "below-chin", "on": "picture", "box": [192, 1003.2, 696, 238.1], "lines": 2, "font": 96, "style": "text", "align": "center"}],
                 "problems": [], "hard": 0, "soft": 0, "unmet": [], "ground": 0, "faceHeight": 540,
                 "terms": {"enlargement": 0, "ground": 0, "subject": 0, "soft": 0, "text": 0, "hook": 0}, "cost": 0, "score": 100,
                 "why": ["no hard problem", "every text need met: 1 caption line below-chin, hook text at 96 px below-chin", "the source's pixels are not enlarged (1×)",
                         "the face is 540 px tall (28 % of the frame's height)", "the picture fills the frame", "cost 0 (no term)"]}],
 "defaults": {}, "asked": {"frame": "9:16", "platforms": []}}
```

- `scale` is frame px per source px; `left`, `top` where the source's top left corner lies in the frame; `window` the part of the frame the sharp picture fills (the rest is ground). `stacked` and `side-by-side` carry `parts`, one such placement per half; a screen with a webcam a `circle` (its `box` in px of the frame, and `crop`, the square it shows, in px of the picture `of` names: `webcam`, its own file, or `screen`, the screen recording a webcam is baked into).
- `problems` are hard (`✗`: under what an app covers, out of the frame, text on a face or on text, a face cut by its picture's edge, the source enlarged over the forbidden limit, the circle on the region), soft (`!`: in the safe box's margin, cut by a feed's crop, enlarged over the soft limit, another person cut by a crop's edge) or unmet (`–`: a text without a free place, a screen's text too small).
- `tracks` (a face that moves): its usual box, its hull, how much larger the hull is, which moment gives the hull each edge, and `decides`: the moments without which the first candidate would be another or placed otherwise, each with `says`, what changes in words.
- `onGround` (a candidate of its own, named `…, text on the ground`): where the picture stands smaller than an upright frame, the same picture with its hook and captions on the ground above and below it. `groundShows`: what the ground is (the pages' blurred, darker copy of the picture, 1.17× larger than covering the frame) and, for each text on it, whether a face of the picture lies there as a blur.
- `alike`: candidates that showed the same frame to the eye and are listed once, as this one (their fit, scale and how many px they lay from it).
- `notes` say what no candidate can; a candidate's own `notes` say that its caption area is narrower than a long word.

`page` writes `candidate-N.html` and `candidate-N.png` for the first three candidates (or `--candidate 2`, `1,4`, `all`) and, with `--still`, `still.jpg`: one frame of the recording, without any of the file's tags. A face that moves gets a picture of every candidate at every moment: `--still <video>` takes the moments its boxes were read at, or the `--at` given (several allowed); pictures of one source go one `--at` each, in their order. The files carry the moment (`candidate-1-at-2.5.png`, `still-at-2.5.jpg`). Two sources (two people, a screen and its webcam) take a `--still` each, in their order (`still-2.jpg`): the webcam's circle and panel show its picture, cut as the candidate says; without its still the circle is drawn. The picture is taken in a window taller than the frame and cut back to it, so that it is the frame with any Chrome (one that counts its own bars into the window left the lowest rows black). What `page` wrote is listed in `placement.json` (`written`): the next `page` or `place` into the folder removes exactly those files, never another one, and never a file that is passed in (a still that already lies in the folder is used as it is; a file of the caller's under the name of a frame page would write is refused, not written over). `grid` writes the one picture it is given a name for, and refuses to write over the file it reads. Nothing is written into the skill.

## Two checks

The search judges thousands of placements, too many to open a page for each: `tools/lib/judge.mjs` applies the rules of platform-zones' layout check to the solver's own boxes, with the letters' place estimated from the font's metrics. `page` then has platform-zones itself measure the candidate's page in Chrome, by its letters, and prints both verdicts side by side. The checker takes 2 px off every side of a box before it judges, and so does the solver: a box up to 2 px past an edge counts as on it for both. They agree on every page of the tests (about 120) and on the sweep's winners (below). On 320 random inputs (every frame, a size as `720x1280`, sources of nine shapes, one or two faces, a moving face, two files, a screen, named platforms) all 1,017 candidates as pages got the same verdict from both; one finding differed, on a candidate that fails anyway (a face 0.65 px past the frame's edge after the slack, which the page rounds to a whole px). The letters' place is an estimate within about 3 px: on a 720×1280 frame the checker found a hook text 2 px into a feed's crop where the solver found it clear, a soft finding, the verdict the same. What only the solver judges, because the checker cannot know it: how far the source is enlarged, a face cut by its own picture's edge, a text need without a place, a screen's text too small, another person showing in a crop or a half, the circle on the region.

## The weights

Three things rank before any cost. A candidate with a hard problem stands behind every one without. One that leaves a need unmet stands behind every one that meets them all. And where no speaker is named, a crop that leaves a person out of the picture stands behind every candidate that shows them all: whom to leave out is the caller's decision (`--speaker`), no weight makes it. Among the rest the smaller cost wins, in points, from [`tools/weights.json`](tools/weights.json) (every term with a sentence of what it says); `--weights file.json` takes another file, `--set weights.enlarge.softFrom=1.4` changes one term.

| term | value | what it does | where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `enlarge.softFrom` | 1.5× | up to here enlarging the source costs nothing | judged: 1.5× looked crisp |
| `enlarge.forbiddenFrom` | 2× | over this a candidate fails | a guess between 1.875× (judged fine) and 2.67× (judged too soft) |
| `enlarge.costAtForbidden` | 3 points | the cost at the limit, rising in a straight line from `softFrom` | set so that the judged order comes out; from about 3.3 the crisp square would pass the mildly soft 4:5 crop, and nobody judged which of the two should lead |
| `subject.from`, `subject.to` | 23 %, 34 % | a face this tall, in per cent of the frame's height, costs nothing | measured: the middle half of the faces in 49 talking heads and 60 podcast clips of 240 published Shorts (medians 26 % and 29 %) |
| `subject.smaller` | 0.3 a % | for every per cent a face falls short of the band | set so that the judged order comes out |
| `subject.larger` | 0.05 a % | for every per cent a face is over the band; no reward for a larger face | a guess, kept small: a close take zoomed in a little stands before the same take made smaller over a ground |
| `subject.regionEnough` | 50 % | a screen's region is the better the more of the frame it spans, up to this | a guess, no case decides it |
| `ground.cost` | 0.1 a % | for every per cent of the frame the sharp picture does not fill | set so that the judged order comes out |
| `soft.cost` | 0.5 | for a soft problem other than enlargement | a guess |
| `text.away`, `text.hookSmaller` | 0.05, 0.02 | for a text away from its usual place, a hook under its size | small on purpose: they only order holds of the same picture |

**They rest on one judged case, one measurement and guesses.** The case: a 1280×720 take of one person into 9:16 with two caption lines, stills looked at by people: the 4:5 crop at 1.875× first, the square at 1.5× second, the whole picture as a strip third (the person tiny), the crop that fills the frame at 2.67× too soft to publish. The two crops are a quarter of a point apart. The measurement: how tall a face is in published Shorts. It replaced a reward for a larger face up to half the frame's height, which nothing published supports. It says what creators do, not what works: in that study nothing separates the videos that did well from the others (5 of 69 comparisons under p = 0.05, 3.5 expected by chance). There is no term for how much of the picture a crop cuts away. Candidates less than a point apart are not told apart by anything measured.

**Some figures rest on the first use on real footage** (2026-11-18: an agent placed twelve recordings by the documents alone, and what it found wrong was fixed). They are defaults in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`, each with its source, and rest on these twelve cases only:

- `insetFace` (19 %): an inset goes below half the picture's size (`insetMin`) while its smallest face stays this tall; the lower end of the middle 80 % of faces in published talking heads, not one of these cases. A take with a face half its height, a hook and two caption lines passes only so.
- `free.above` (0.35): what is kept clear of text above a face's box, a share of its height, for the top of the head. On the stills the head's top lay 0.22, 0.23, 0.31 and 0.335 of the box's height above it (bald and frontal, short hair, grey hair in profile, bald in profile: there a hook card had ended on the skull); hair worn high reached 0.49 and is not held. The upright take laid out by hand has a box read from the top of the head, so its cases keep the margin it has (`hand.json`). With this margin two people stack less often: a face over about a third of a half's height no longer fits a half with the captions on the seam.
- `same` (2 % of the frame's shorter side, 2 % of scale): two candidates whose pictures, windows, circles and text lie this close are one; the later is named in the first's `alike` and the next different one takes its place. Pairs 9 and 18 px apart looked the same in their pictures, pairs 193 and 218 px apart did not. `--all` lists them all.
- `together` (2), `togetherSize` (0.67): two people of one picture stand close together when their faces are about the same size (the smaller at least two thirds of the larger: at the same distance from the camera), their boxes at most two mean face widths apart across and at most one face's height up or down. Stacked would split them into two halves of the same shot; it stands behind every candidate that shows both in one picture (walking side by side: 1.0–2.3 widths; four in a row with room between them: 2.2–3.8; two at a table: 12.9).

The type sizes come from the same study (a caption line 4 % of the frame's height, one line; a hook text 12.4 % of the height tall in the middle of the frame): they are defaults in `tools/lib/defaults.mjs`, each with its source. [`references/situations.md`](references/situations.md) has the figures with their counts.

### The sweep

`node tools/sweep.mjs <folder> [--weights mine.json] [--no-check] [--jobs N]` (`--jobs 1`: one checker and one Chrome at a time, for a machine busy otherwise) is for whoever changes the weights or the defaults. It runs the solver on 16,128 made-up faces (15–55 % of the source's height, the eyes at 25–60 % of it, up to 15 % of the width off the middle; an upright and a wide source into each of the four frames; one caption line, two, a hook, a hook and two lines), has platform-zones check every winner's boxes and 384 winners as pages, and writes `<folder>/sweep.json` (9 MB) and `<folder>/sweep.html`: one map per case and text need of which placement wins for which face, the thresholds in words, and, with `--weights`, how many winners the other weights change against the skill's own and from what to what. Two to five minutes on a machine with 20 cores, ten seconds without the checker; `--html` only rebuilds the page.

It says what the weights do, not what looks good. Moderate changes move little: with enlargement at 5 points at the limit instead of 3, no winner of the grid changed.

## How far it has been tested

- **Two takes laid out by hand** ([`tools/hand.json`](tools/hand.json): the numbers of 14 layouts, no picture). An upright phone take (2026-11-16), filmed unusually close (the face 47 % of the frame's height, the chin at 77 %; published talking heads keep the face at about a quarter and the chin at half height): text above the head is the answer for this take, not the general rule. With the hand's type (56 px, no box around the captions) and its hook as a card the solver finds the hand's zoom of 1.07 at the same place, the inset that holds the hook card at its size (0.82), and the panel in 16:9. A wide 1280×720 take (2026-11-17): it proposes the hand's three crops at the same place. For 25 cases of these takes the first candidate under the skill's own defaults is written down with who stands behind it: 5 the hand's layout, 1 judged by people, 19 only the solver's. The study's figures and the room for a caption box's padding moved 8 of the first 21: with the larger type two caption lines no longer fit above the head of the close take in the zoom, and the wide take in 4:5 takes the crisp square band, not the frame filled at 1.875×.
- **`tools/test.mjs`**: 62 tests, about two minutes with Chrome, a few seconds with `--quick` (52 tests, no Chrome, no ffmpeg). Every page the tests write gets the same findings from the solver and the checker.
- **An independent review** (2026-11-17) checked 3,100 candidates from 876 inputs against its own geometry (coverage, faces, zones, every moment of a moving face) and could not break them. What it did find is fixed, each with a test on its input: a source wider than the frame's shape was never tried smaller than the frame's width; inputs could be deleted; a Chrome with bars in its window gave pictures ending in black; a frame of another size kept the type of 1080; the caption area did not hold the padding of a style's box; a soft crop on one of two people stood before both shown; old pages stayed beside a new placement; four inputs gave stack traces or ran without end. A check of those fixes on the review's own inputs (2026-11-17) found three more, fixed and tested: `page` wrote its frame over a `still.jpg` of the caller's lying in the folder, a search step of a millionth ran for minutes, and a face box past the source's edge was placed as if the face were there.
- **A second independent review** (2026-11-18, of the fixes after the first use on real footage) checked 330 random inputs, 1,353 candidate pages (no verdict apart from the checker's) and 3,742 passing candidates (no text on a face or in its margins, nothing over 2×, nothing off the frame). What it found is fixed, each with a test on its input: a named speaker's crop was sized by a bystander's face; the inset stopped at half the take's size where a smaller one held a hook and two caption lines (all 63 cells the sweep had left unmet); screen-stacked kept the webcam at 1.5× with its face under the bars, pinned a phone's screen to the frame's edge, and printed a negative height; a near person and a far one counted as standing close; a moving face's pictures showed its hull as if it were the face; and seven small ones (no zoom closer from a 4K source of the frame's shape, the take in the middle of 16:9 at one scale only, a closer crop's heads on the safe box's edges, a sliver of a face with two findings, no note for screen-stacked in 4:5, an error for too many stills that named no fix, whose px a baked webcam's crop is in).
- **The sweep** (2026-11-18, after the fixes of the first use on real footage and of the review of them): no winner with a hard problem, none leaves a need unmet (14 did on 2026-11-17, 63 after the margin above the head was kept: an inset below half the take's size, while its face stays 19 % of the frame tall, now holds a hook and two caption lines beside a face half the take tall). A quarter of the winners changed against 2026-11-17, nearly all by that margin (smaller insets and bands where text stood above the head), 449 upright takes in 16:9 by the picture in the middle; the fixes of the second review moved 217 more (upright takes in 16:9 to the smaller picture in the middle, the 63 cells that had no place, 63 wide takes in a square). Of the 16,128 winners' boxes none fails the checker and all agree in every finding; 384 as pages in Chrome: all agree (run with `--jobs 1`).
- **Drawn placeholders** in the tests for several faces, a face that moves and a screen: their pages show ovals and a drawn surface. Three or more people in parts are not built.
- **On real footage**: the two takes above, as stills; the upright one also as four short videos, built by hand before this skill existed. And twelve cases placed by an agent from the documents alone (2026-11-18, stills of public b-roll, interviews, a talk show in 4K, a talk with slides, our own desktop recording with a webcam): a wide talking head into 9:16, 4:5 and 1:1, an upright one into 9:16 and 16:9, two people with and without a speaker, two walking, four walking, a desktop, slides with the speaker baked in, a screen and a webcam as files. What it found wrong (a stacked pair that walked side by side, a face past the safe box that passed, a hook card on a bald skull, the speaker twice, one picture in two sizes, duplicates, no crop closer from 4K, no screen above a face, no text on the ground, no upright take in the middle of 16:9, and five documents or messages) is fixed with a test each; after the fixes the first candidate is what an editor would take in all twelve. Not yet: a real upright phone take, a phone's screen recording, a named platform, and a placement built into a video by another skill.
- Named platforms (`--platform`) were tried by hand on the upright take only: for Instagram Reels alone it passes unchanged, as the hand had found, and with TikTok named as well it takes the zoom. Every test but one and the sweep run with the frames' usual platforms.

Beside screencast-cut's own layout for a screen with a webcam (its code run on the same case on 2026-11-17, before the type sizes changed: a 1920×1080 screen, a region of 480×300 with 14 px text, 9:16, two caption lines of 56 px): screencast-cut scales what matters as large as its room allows, up to 2× (1.48×, the text 20.7 px); this solver takes the smallest scale that reads (1.71×, 24 px) and says when that cannot be had. screencast-cut pads the region by 10 % and never shows less than 38 % of the screen's width; this takes the region as given. screencast-cut's circle always stands bottom left in 9:16, above a band of 216 px kept for captions whatever their lines (y 962–1200); this one stands above the lines asked for (y 1030–1268) and moves to another corner, or shrinks, when the region is there.

## Text over a clip placed already (`text`)

A clip of several shots whose picture another skill placed (clips' framing: a window on whoever speaks, shots shown whole, two panels) needs no candidates for the picture, only where its text goes, given every face over time. `text` takes the shots (`--shots shots.json`: each `start`, `end`, `weight` (its seconds in the cut), `faces` as tracks of `{ t, box, feat? }` in px of the frame (`feat`: the box of eyes, nose and mouth), `room` how far its picture may be raised without a ground, `null` for none at all) and the hook's time, and gives back (`<folder>/text.json`, `lib/placed.mjs`):

- **One place for the captions of the whole clip.** A caption that jumps from shot to shot reads badly, and published Shorts keep theirs fixed within a video (the layout study: the middle at 60–75 % of the height, below the chin in 88 % of the frames with a face, one line of about three words in 77 %). Every height of the safe box in steps of half a per cent, at the usual type (`caption.font`, 77 px in 1920) and at 90, 80 and 70 % of it as long as that is not under `caption.font.least` (61 px: the low end of published captions, a line 3.2 % of the height in the study's 188 Shorts; so 69 and 62 px, not 54), one line (or the lines asked for), centred and as wide as `place` makes them. The area is `caption.line` em a line and `caption.padEm` em more: by default the tallest style's (1.3 and 0.44); a caller that knows the style sets both (clips: the style's line height, and for a style without a box the ink of its outline and shadow below the line), so that the area is what the ink takes.
- **Each shot's lift**: how far its picture goes up so that every face's keep-out (the box and the margins of `free`, the same as `place` keeps) clears the captions at every moment; once for the shot, by its lowest moment. At most what its `room` allows without a ground plus a ground that stays under the apps' bottom (below the safe box), never so far that a face's eyes (`eyes` of its box) reach above the safe box's top, and, by the caller's `--raise`: `head`, not so far that the top of a face's box and the margin above it for the hair (`free.above`) go over the frame's top (a head cut by the frame is a fault of its own); `mouth` (the default; `moderate` is its old name), as head, and past the head's top as far as the eyes allow where the captions would otherwise lie on a mouth or a lower lip and that costs less than the seconds it saves (`past`); `full`, until the chin is clear. Each shot says how far the frame cuts a head at the top (`headCut`: px at most, seconds, and how much of it the lift adds). The returns of one framing (the same face at the same size and place: the camera cut back to a close-up it showed before) stand at one height, that of the one that needs most: a close-up that came back at 144, 363, 225, 97 and 150 px read as a camera that moves.
- **The lines of a face**, from the caller's `feat` (the eyes, nose and mouth, a box per moment beside `box`), else from the box by the head's proportions (`IMPLIED`: the eyes' top at 38 %, the mouth's bottom at 86 % of the box, the low end measured on stills whose features were right, so that a line is never above the real one): the nose line 70 % down `feat`, the lip line 88 % down (the middle of the mouth), the mouth line its bottom and 15 % of its height more (`OPEN`: an open mouth's lower lip drops below the box a model gives of a still, and a head that tips between two stills carries its mouth lower in its box; on the rendered frames of three talk-show clips the lower lip stood up to 7, 8 and 28 % of that height below the box in nine frames of ten). Captions reaching above the mouth line lie on the lower lip (1,000 points a second: a smaller type or another height comes first; what remains at the floor is said as not avoidable at a readable size, a `note:`), above the lip line on the mouth, both lips (20,000 a second: a fault, a conflict, after a raise past the head's top as above), above the nose line on the nose or the eyes (100,000 a second, a conflict); below the mouth line on the chin or neck (a `note:` where no lift may clear it). Above a head, within the margin kept for the hair: said as on the hair.
- **The cost** that ranks the places (`COSTS` in `lib/placed.mjs`, guesses set so that a second of text on a face outweighs the rest): 10 points a second on a face's keep-out, 30 times how deep into the face from its chin the area reaches (a share of the face's height) a second, the parts' points above; raising a shot 1 point a second for every tenth of the frame's height by its window, 4 over a ground; a middle outside 60–75 % 1 point for every per cent and every ten seconds of the clip; smaller type 1 a px and ten seconds. The smallest wins; the next four are listed.
- **A place that changes at a cut** is only proposed (`changes`, a `note:`): for a shot of four seconds or more where another height saves more than two seconds on a face. Taking it is the caller's.
- **The hook** over its seconds on every shot it spans, raised as they are, beside the captions: `layoutText`'s own kinds and order (a text below the chin, above the head, a card at the top), its keep-outs the faces of every moment in its time; where the caller says the picture behind it is not calm (`"hook": {"calm": false}`: it knows the faces, not hands or things), a text's place takes a card, also where no place is free. Where no place is free and the first shot changes within its time, it ends at that cut (from 1.2 s on); else the least bad of its usual places, said with why (`kindWhy`).
- **What still lies on a face**: per shot who, from when to when, the seconds on each part (`on`: nose, mouth, lip, chin) and how deep; a conflict (✗) only where something can still be done (a cut to another shot, a smaller type by hand, a larger lift than one fixed, a framing that follows the speaker), a note for what the rules leave. `--caption-y` below the safe box's bottom (where the apps lay their own text) or above its top is refused.

Tested on made-up tracks (`test.mjs`: a face high, low, a tight close-up no lift clears, a close-up raised past its head's top for its mouth, one whose eyes allow no raise (captions over the whole mouth: a conflict, never "the lower lip"), returns of one close-up at one height, two shots that disagree, a shot that cannot be raised, a face that moves under the captions, faces low everywhere, the hook below the chin, the hook's fallback over a busy picture, the hook ending at a cut, the command and its refusals) and on five passages of two channels through clips (2026-11-18, after an independent review; caption ink from Chrome against the parts a model found on the rendered frames): moderately raised, on the nose or eyes 0 s in all five, on the mouth 1.5 s in one talk-show clip (0.5 s in another before, now 0), the type 77 px in all five (62 px in two before) where tight close-ups go up past the head's top, ink outside the safe box 0 s (up to 17.5 s before). The cost's figures are guesses; none was judged against another by people.

## What it cannot do

- **See.** The boxes are an agent's reading of a grid; a wrong box is a wrong layout, with every check passing. Whether the picture behind a text is calm, it never knows.
- **Measure words.** A caption block and a hook are taken as wide as their area; the pages show placeholder words (caption lines of three words, a hook of two short lines). The video's own words are checked where the video is built.
- **Follow a face.** A moving face gets one placement for the whole clip, by its hull; following it, or cutting the clip where it moves, is only suggested in a note. Moments that give the hull no edge are not tried as deciding.
- **Time.** `place` lays out one moment of a video; `text` places a clip's text over time where the picture is placed already, but never chooses the picture of a shot. Which layout a part gets, how long a hook stays and what changes when it leaves are the agent's.
- **Name tags, step texts, a second picture** (material above a person): not placed. `stacked` is two people.
- **Other subjects than faces and screens.** A product, a hand, a thing shown: [auto-reframe](../auto-reframe/) follows one moving subject.

## The code

`tools/place.mjs` is the command line. `tools/lib/`: `placed.mjs` (text over a clip placed already, over time), `solve.mjs` (the cases, the ranking and its reasons), `fits.mjs` (the fits of one picture), `parts.mjs` (two people in two halves), `screen.mjs` (a screen and a webcam's circle), `evaluate.mjs` (one placement judged and costed), `text.mjs` (where the text finds room), `judge.mjs` (the zones' rules on the solver's boxes, and the comparison with the checker), `page.mjs` (a candidate as a page), `png.mjs` (a picture cut back to the frame), `zones.mjs` (platform-zones as a program), `defaults.mjs` (what is assumed, and the weights), `boxes.mjs` (the geometry), `sweep-page.mjs` (the sweep's page).
